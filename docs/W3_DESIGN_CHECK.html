The short answer

  • The question as first posed is already answered. Fable asked whether a diffusion network keeps its own clock: take away the label that tells it which denoising step it is on, and does it still know where it is? It does, and trivially. The noise level is visible in the noisy image itself. Sun, Jiang, Zhao and He (ICML 2025) show this for image models, and Lu
    1. shows a linear probe reading the step at every depth of a large model trained with no label. Our own check (§2) agrees: an untrained network reads the step of a noisy image to within a median of 48 steps out of 1,000 on MNIST and 62 on CIFAR-10, and fourteen simple image statistics do better still (23 and 38).
  • What is still open is the conflict. Give a network that does get the label a label that says one step while the image is really at another. Inside the network, does its sense of position follow the label or the content? The output-level answer is “some of both” (Yu et al., CVPR 2026). Nobody has measured it inside the network. That is the program’s standing question (does a system’s internal read follow what it is told, or what is there?), asked of a system with no mouth and an exact right answer at every step.
  • W3 measures it as one number per layer, the split weight w: 1 when the internal read follows the content, 0 when it follows the label. The simulation in §3 shows the statistic is unbiased, and with two gates and a level check it calls every planted world correctly, including the two ways a network can break when pushed off its training distribution. Superseded at registration (§8): the probe statistic is confounded when the label is an exact code and the content a noisy one, which is W3’s situation. The registered primary is a probe-free movement share; the probe statistic stays as a secondary.
  • A label sham checks that a label-following read follows the label’s meaning, not any disturbance of the label channel.
  • The arm itself is cheap: a few thousand forward passes. The cost is training the models, one to three T4 hours, set by a five-minute timing smoke that is built and staged.

2. How much of the clock is in the input

windows/power/w3_design_check.py (§1; outputs w3_design_check_s1_{mnist,cifar10}.{txt,json}). For 6,000 images per dataset, noise is added at a random step, and a ridge probe is trained on 4,000 of them to read the noise level (log-SNR) back. It is scored on the other 2,000. Three kinds of features, none from a trained network:

  • pixels: the raw noisy image;
  • hand: 14 numbers per image, its overall spread, edge energy, and power in 8 frequency rings;
  • random-init net: the encoder of the candidate U-Net with random weights and no label, mean-pooled at its mid-block (three seeds; they agree within .01).

Cosine schedule (the one W3 would use); R² of the read step, and the median error in steps out of 1,000:

features MNIST R² MNIST median error CIFAR-10 R² CIFAR-10 median error
pixels .930 57 .082 229
hand (14 numbers) .984 23 .948 38
random-init net .941 48 .879 62

Median error by band of steps, CIFAR-10, cosine:

steps 0–50 50–100 100–200 200–400 400–600 600–800 800–900 900–1000
hand 6 14 26 45 46 69 30 38
random-init net 26 18 46 58 126 80 33 112

What this settles:

  • The position is in the input. Fourteen summary numbers read the step of a noisy CIFAR image to a median of 38 steps out of 1,000. Any claim that a network “knows where it is” from a matched input has to beat that, or it has found the input. The floor is the hand statistics, the stronger of the two baselines.
  • Raw pixels are no floor. On natural images the noise level is a property of the pixels’ spread, not of any one pixel, and a linear read of raw pixels misses it (R² .08). A pixel probe would make every network look like it keeps a clock.
  • The input is a weaker witness in the middle of the process. For the random-init network the error is 126 steps around steps 400–600 and 18–33 steps near the ends. If the trained network leans on the label anywhere, the middle is where to expect it. That is why the split is also reported by band.
  • For the split arm, the content carries strong evidence of position. A pure LABEL verdict would mean a network ignoring evidence that a 14-number summary can read. That expectation is written down here, before any training.

3. The split weight and its teeth

windows/power/w3_split_check.py (outputs w3_split_check_output.{txt,json}). Simulation only.

The measurement. Each test image is noised to two steps, t and t′ = t + offset (offsets of 25, 100 and 300 steps), and shown to the network four ways: content at t with the label t, content at t′ with the label t′ (the matched pair), and the two mismatches, content at t′ with label t and content at t with label t′. A probe trained only on matched inputs reads the position at a layer. The split weight is

w = slope through the origin of [ read(content c, label l) − g(level of l) ] on [ g(level of c) − g(level of l) ]

where g is the probe’s own calibration curve on matched inputs. w = 1: the read sits where the content puts it. w = 0: it sits where the label puts it. Two choices keep w honest:

  • g instead of the true level. A ridge probe shrinks its reads toward the middle. g measures that shrinkage, and w is scored against it.
  • A fitted g, not each image’s own matched reads, in the denominator. Noisy reads in the denominator would pull w toward 0, which is “label”, an error that flatters one answer.

The verdict at the mid-block, from 95% bootstrap intervals over images: CONTENT (lower end ≥ .8), LABEL (upper end ≤ .2), SPLIT (the whole interval inside .2–.8), UNCLEAR otherwise. Two gates and a level check come first.

  • Tracking gate. Fit the mismatched reads on both levels at once, read = α + a·g(c) + b·g(l). For any mix of content and label, a + b = 1. For reads that have stopped being positions, a + b = 0. If the interval for a + b reaches below .8, the verdict is UNREADABLE.
  • Precision gate. If the mismatched reads scatter around the fitted mix more than 1.5 times as much as matched reads scatter around g, the verdict is UNREADABLE.
  • Level check. w is also fitted within four bands of steps. If two bands get different decisive verdicts with non-overlapping intervals, the verdict is VARIES BY LEVEL, and the band verdicts are the finding.

The first pass found three holes, all fixed before the run below. (1) A network whose mismatched reads collapse to the middle was called SPLIT .5 when the probe was noisy (that is why the tracking gate exists). (2) A network that follows the label at some levels and the content at others came out UNREADABLE or SPLIT (that is why the level check exists). (3) The sham statistic, pooled over offsets, was swamped by the 25-step class (§4).

Planted worlds (200 simulated experiments per cell, 500 bootstrap draws each). σ is the probe’s read noise in log-SNR units. For scale, the input-level probes of §2 have about 0.85–1.1 on MNIST; a trained network’s probe should do no worse.

  • False VARIES in the five worlds with no level dependence: at most 1.5% of experiments in any cell.
  • Choice: 256 test images per offset class. It holds every world at ≥ .95 even at σ = 2. The cost is forward passes, a few seconds.

4. The label sham

The question it answers: if the read follows the label, does it follow the label’s meaning (a different step), or does it move whenever the label channel is disturbed at all? For each image, the label vector is moved by a random direction with the same length as the real move from step t to step t′. A deterministic network reads the same input the same way every time, so any shift under the sham is caused by the sham.

Statistic, per offset class: S = mean |shift under the sham| / mean |shift under the real label move|, with the content held fixed. SILENT when the 95% upper bound of S is ≤ 0.25 on the 300-step class, the primary. S is judged only when the label moves the read at all (verdict LABEL, SPLIT or VARIES).

Simulated with the sham moving the read k times as far as the real move, plus incidental jitter (“shift noise”, log-SNR units), 128 images per class:

real w shift noise k = 0 k = .1 k = .3 k = .5
0 .05 SILENT 1.00 1.00 0.00 0.00
0 .25 1.00 1.00 0.00 0.00
0 .50 1.00 1.00 0.00 0.00
.5 .05 1.00 1.00 0.00 0.00
.5 .25 1.00 .99 0.00 0.00
.5 .50 0.00 0.00 0.00 0.00

(The rate shown is P(SILENT) on the 300-step class.)

  • It separates a quiet sham (k ≤ .1) from a reactive one (k ≥ .3) cleanly, as long as the jitter is small next to the real shift.
  • It fails safe. When the jitter is large next to the real shift (w = .5, jitter .5), it withholds SILENT even from a quiet sham. A noisy network can’t earn SILENT, and it can’t fake it either.
  • Why only the 300-step class. On the 25-step class the real move is tiny, so any jitter dominates (S .51–.93 at k = 0 with jitter ≥ .25). Scored there, the sham would measure noise, not reaction. The 25- and 100-step classes are reported, not judged.

  • Proposed: CIFAR-10 at width 64 (6.5 M parameters), about 49 epochs per T4 hour. The label costs nothing measurable in speed, so the twins train in equal time.
  • Training dominates the cost. The split arm, the sham and the calibration together are about 5,000 forward passes per model, about 40 batches, under 5 seconds. Seed-spread sampling (8 continuations × 50 steps × a few hundred states) is minutes.
  • Total: three models at about 1.2 T4 hours each (60 epochs), about 4 T4 hours. That is a small fraction of the 473 compute credits Joe has.
  • The FLOP extrapolation was optimistic. windows/power/w3_budget_check.py counts 8.7 GFLOP per image per training step at width 64. The measured 0.187 s per step of 128 images is about 6 TFLOP/s effective. The assumed range was 8–15, so the extrapolation promised 66–125 epochs per hour against the 49 measured. The registration uses the measurement.
  • The first run of the smoke failed on a bug that the local CPU test could not catch: the label encoder built a tensor on the CPU. The bug was fixed at the source, then checked by running every configuration forward and backward on PyTorch’s meta device, which rejects any stray CPU tensor. The second run is the one above.

6. The design this check proposes for registration

Substrate. The §5 U-Net at 32 × 32: width 64, multipliers 1-2-2, two residual blocks per level, attention at 8 × 8 and in the mid-block. Cosine schedule, T = 1,000, trained to predict the noise. CIFAR-10, because the content is a weaker witness of position there than on MNIST, and weakest in the middle of the process (§2). Every outcome has room.

Three models, with the same architecture, seed and data order, trained for the same number of epochs:

  • M-on: gets the label (the step, encoded and added inside every residual block). This is the primary, the ordinary design in the field.
  • M-drop: the same, but the label is replaced by a learned “no label” vector on 20% of training examples. Secondary. A missing label is then something the network has seen in training, not a broken input.
  • M-off: no label at all. Secondary. It replicates the known result (Sun et al. 2025; Lu
    1. on our own substrate.

Training gate, before any probe: in every band of steps, each model’s held-out noise prediction must beat both trivial predictors (all zeros, and the noisy input itself). A probe is never read on a network that has not learned to denoise.

Probes. Ridge regression on mean-pooled activations at every resolution level of the encoder, at the mid-block (the primary site) and at every level of the decoder. Target: log-SNR. Probes are trained only on matched inputs (content level = label level) from training images, calibrated (g) on a separate split, and scored on held-out test images.

Arms.

  1. Input floor. The hand-statistics probe and each model’s random-init twin, on the same images, reported by band. M-on’s reads of matched inputs are calibration, not findings, because the label is part of the input.
  2. The split (primary). M-on; offsets of 25, 100 and 300 steps, both directions; 256 test images per offset class; the same noise draw across each image’s 2 × 2. The statistic is w at the mid-block, with the gates and verdicts of §3: CONTENT · LABEL · SPLIT · VARIES BY LEVEL · UNCLEAR · UNREADABLE.
  3. The label sham. On the 300-step class: the label moved by a random vector the size of the real move. S ≤ 0.25 means SILENT. Judged when the primary verdict is LABEL, SPLIT or VARIES; reported otherwise.

Secondaries (named in advance, no multiplicity claim):

  • w layer by layer, a profile through the U-Net;
  • w by direction (content noisier than the label vs cleaner);
  • whether w predicts the output’s error, which is Yu et al.’s output-level measure, read here as the noise level implied by the network’s own prediction;
  • M-drop: w against M-on’s w, and its no-label reads against the content;
  • M-off: its read against the hand-statistics floor (replicating Lu 2026’s “hidden states beat input statistics”);
  • seed spread: within narrow step bands, a probe from one run’s mid-block to the spread of the final images across 8 continuations from the same state, against the Tweedie-moment baseline.

What it can and cannot show. It can show which source a small denoiser’s internal read of its own position follows when the two disagree, layer by layer and level by level. It cannot show anything about persistence or stakes, because the network keeps nothing between generations. The result is about this class of network at this size.

7. Decisions, settled 2026-10-11

Joe, on reading the recommendations: “as usual I am good with your recommendations.” The registration carries:

  • All three models fly together: M-on (primary), M-drop (no-label vector on 20% of examples), M-off. Credits are not the constraint (473 on hand), and M-drop is the cleanest test of a missing label.
  • About 60 epochs each, about 1.2 T4 hours per model at the measured 49 epochs per hour, about 4 T4 hours in all. The exact number goes in the registration. The training gate guards the floor whatever the number.
  • Seed spread stays a pre-named secondary. Guidance strength and the inversion control are deferred to their own pieces.

8. Added at registration: the collinearity check

windows/power/w3_collinear_check.py (outputs w3_collinear_check_output.{txt,json}), written 2026-10-11 while drafting the registration, before anything was trained.

The flaw. The §3 probe is trained only on matched inputs, where the content level and the label level are the same number. A feature that tracks the label and a feature that tracks the content are then perfectly collinear in its training data, and ridge regression divides its weight between them by how cleanly each predicts the target. A label code is exact. A content code reads the level with per-image error: §2’s fourteen statistics leave R² .84 of log-SNR on CIFAR-10. The probe can lean on the label while the representation moves mostly with the content. §3’s planted worlds could not show this, because they planted the reads, not the activations behind them.

The check plants activation worlds (an additive content code, label code, image variation and noise-draw variation, 64 features) and runs the §3 probe statistic beside a probe-free one, the movement share: of the network’s own move between the matched inputs at t and t′, the share that moving the content alone produces, measured along that move, pooled over both directions. Pooled that way it is the content’s Shapley share of the move, and the label’s share is exactly one minus it. Its gate is κ, the energy of the 2 × 2 interaction over the energy of the move; the level check is §3’s, with a floor (band shares at least 0.2 apart) because the movement share’s intervals are tight.

What it settles.

  • The probe statistic fails where W3 will be. With a content code that misreads the level by about the input floor and a label code 0.3 its size, the probe calls LABEL in every run, while the representation moves 91% with the content (MISREAD.3). With equal codes it calls LABEL where the truth is half and half (MISREAD-EQ). It measures which source gives the cleanest position code: a real quantity, but not the question.
  • The movement share calls every world right, including the collapse and scatter failures (κ 30 and 5.6 against .03–.05 for additive worlds and .16 for the interacting one; the bar is 0.5) and both level-dependent worlds.
  • The registration’s primary is the movement share at the mid-block, with the same verdict bars (0.2 / 0.8), the κ gate and the level check. The probe statistic is secondary S2, and the registration pre-reads what a disagreement between the two means.
  • Two rules changed during the check: a world whose true share sat on the 0.2 bar was called VARIES for band shares of .16 and .24, which added the 0.2 floor (a 0.3 floor, tried first, cost the banded world its real .72-versus-.98 contrast); and the interacting world’s right verdict is VARIES, since its label effect changes with level by construction.
