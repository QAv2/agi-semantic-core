# Prior art: does the loop know where it is?

Source design: `docs/IDEATION_WINDOWS_2026-10-10.md`, section 3, card "### 2. Does the loop know where it is?".

Terms used below:
- **Noise level / position.** The variance of the noise mixed into the current image x_t. In a DDPM it is set by the step index t through the schedule; "position in the process" and "noise level" mean the same thing here.
- **Clock.** The timestep embedding, the extra input that tells the network t.
- **Noise-unconditional (blind) model.** A denoiser trained with no clock input at all.
- **Probe.** A linear (ridge or logistic) readout trained on frozen activations.
- **Input-statistics baseline.** The same probe fitted on simple statistics of x_t itself (pixel variance, high-frequency energy, and so on) instead of on network activations.

## Short answer

- **The clock-off arm is already answered in substance, and the answer is "yes, and trivially so".** Sun, Jiang, Zhao and He (ICML 2025) show the posterior over noise level given x_t is sharply peaked for images (variance about t²/2d in their flow-matching analysis, d = pixel count). A separate 30K-parameter CNN predicts the noise level with mean error about 0.01. Diffusion models trained with no clock at all lose little sample quality, and some improve. A probe reading position from content is therefore expected, not discovered.
- **The image-domain probe itself has been done, in a 2026 preprint.** Lu (arXiv 2605.06169, Appendix H) trained a 400-layer rectified-flow transformer with no timestep embedding and found that a linear probe on the token mean predicts t with near-perfect R² at every depth, and that hidden states remove nearly all the error left by scalar input statistics. The language-model analog has been done twice in 2026 (Rulli et al., arXiv 2607.01774, EMNLP 2026; the "Induction in Both Directions" preprint, arXiv 2607.15893), both with causal steering of the inferred time.
- **The displacement arm is partly answered at the output level.** Yu et al. (CVPR 2026, arXiv 2604.16044) hold the clock fixed and feed images of other noise levels: the network's noise prediction grows with the actual noise, so it responds to content, but its predictions are "significantly inaccurate" under mismatch, so it also leans on the label. The exposure-bias line (Ning et al. ICML 2023 and ICLR 2024; Li et al. ICLR 2024) shows sampled states drift off the schedule and that feeding the network the timestep matching the actual variance improves samples. Nobody I found has asked this at the level of internal activations.
- **The framing has a structural problem the prior art makes visible.** A DDPM step is memoryless: the only thing that survives from one step to the next is x_t itself. "Swap in the state from step t′" therefore just means "evaluate the network on a t′-level input with a t label". In a clock-off model there is no schedule anywhere inside the network for the probe to follow, so the "reads the schedule" outcome is impossible by construction. In a clock-on model, "schedule versus truth" reduces to "label versus content". The experiment should be stated that way.
- **What is still open, as far as this sweep found:** (a) a label-versus-content dissociation measured inside a clock-on image U-Net, with the internal read decomposed into t and t′ weights; (b) whether a single run's activations encode seed-ensemble spread (no direct prior work found; trajectory-variance and Tweedie-moment work are adjacent); (c) whether activations encode the classifier-free-guidance disagreement magnitude (no prior work found).
- **Design changes forced:** add an input-statistics baseline that every positive must beat; replace "hold t constant or scramble it at inference" with a model trained without a clock (plus, ideally, one trained with clock dropout); recast displacement as a 2×2 of content level × label level; keep the sham. Details in the last section.
- **Confidence.** High that clock-off is answered (several independent, verified sources). Medium that no prior work probes ensemble spread or guidance magnitude from activations (two searches each; absence is weak evidence).

## Q1. Noise-level inference without timestep conditioning

- **Sun, Jiang, Zhao, He (2025).** Verified. Their Statement 1 gives Var[t] ≈ t*²/(2d) for the flow-matching posterior, small when the dimension d is large. Checked empirically on AFHQ-v2 64×64. On CIFAR-10, removing the clock costs little for most families (e.g. EDM Heun 1.99 to 3.36 FID; flow matching Euler 3.01 to 2.61, slightly better). The one catastrophic case is DDIM with the deterministic sampler (3.99 to 40.90). Their error bound predicts which models fail. Section 6.2 and Appendix B.2: conditioning on a separately trained noise-level predictor (two conv layers, global pooling, linear head, under 30K parameters, MSE about 1e-4, mean error about 0.01) performs about the same as no conditioning at all, which the authors read as both sharing the same error from estimating t. Their noise-unconditional "uEDM" reaches FID 2.23 on CIFAR-10. They do not probe the internal features of the blind network.
- **Chávez (2026), "On the Redundancy of Timestep Embeddings in Diffusion Models", arXiv 2606.20416.** Verified as a single-author preprint, no venue. U-Net and DiT trained with and without the timestep embedding on CelebA 128 and CIFAR-10. The time-agnostic versions match or beat their conditioned twins (e.g. CIFAR-10 class-conditional DiT FID 51.3 conditioned versus 34.7 without). Absolute FIDs are poor (short training), which is useful: it is roughly the quality regime a one-hour T4 model will be in. No probing of hidden states.
- **Mohan, Kadkhodaie, Simoncelli, Fernandez-Granda (2020), ICLR, arXiv 1906.05478.** Verified. Blind denoisers (no noise-level input) overfit their training noise range; removing all bias terms makes them generalize across noise levels and makes the network locally linear, which allows linear-algebra analysis of what it does at each noise level.
- **Kadkhodaie and Simoncelli (2021), NeurIPS, arXiv 2007.13640.** Verified. Sampling from the prior implicit in a blind denoiser, with step size and injected noise "jointly and adaptively controlled by the denoiser". In practice the effective noise level is estimated from the norm of the denoiser's residual at each step (my reading of the method; the exact formula was not re-checked in this sweep). This is the oldest working example of a loop that sets its own position from content.
- **Kadkhodaie, Guth, Simoncelli, Mallat (2024), ICLR, arXiv 2310.02557.** Verified as to title, venue and main claim. Whether their denoisers are blind was not confirmed in this sweep (unverified, though I believe they follow the bias-free blind lineage).
- **Zheng et al. (2025), ICLR, arXiv 2409.02908.** Verified. Masked diffusion models are theoretically time-agnostic: for discrete masking, the mask fraction carries the time.
- **Text diffusion, the same question already asked.** Rulli et al. (2026), arXiv 2607.01774, verified: LLaDA-1.5 and Dream-7B, no timestep input, MLP probes R² above 0.5 at all layers; linear probes work in early and middle layers but collapse in the last ones; mean-swap steering at layers 29 and 25 shifts confidence and entropy; a norm-matched random direction is the control. The paper does not decouple actual noise from the schedule. "Induction in Both Directions", arXiv 2607.15893 (authors not recorded in this sweep): six small DLMs with no timestep embedding; probe R² about 0 at the input embedding (one token carries no global mask rate) and 0.82–0.91 after layer-0 attention; patching the direction causally changes prediction entropy.
- **Bottom line for Q1.** Networks infer noise level from content readily and accurately; the information is in the input statistics already; the representation is linearly decodable inside at least one large blind image model and several text models.

## Q2. Linear probes and representation analyses on diffusion features

- **Baranchuk et al. (2022), ICLR, arXiv 2112.03126.** Verified. DDPM decoder activations, chosen by block and timestep, give pixel features for few-label segmentation. Shows mid/decoder features are rich and timestep-dependent.
- **Chen, Viégas, Wattenberg (2023), NeurIPS, arXiv 2306.05720.** Verified. Linear probes on Stable Diffusion self-attention activations read depth and salient-object masks; these emerge early in denoising; intervening on them edits output. Code and probe weights public (github.com/yc015/scene-representation-diffusion-model). The closest method template for the SD 1.5 replication arm.
- **Tang et al. (2023), DIFT, NeurIPS, arXiv 2306.03881.** Verified. Features at a chosen (t, layer) give semantic correspondence. The choice of t matters a great deal, which is indirect evidence that activations change strongly and systematically with t.
- **Kwon, Jeong, Uh (2023), ICLR, arXiv 2210.10960.** Verified. The U-Net bottleneck ("h-space", the mid-block output) is a semantic latent space, "consistent across timesteps". This is the same layer the design plans to probe. It suggests semantic content and position may be separable there.
- **Park et al. (2023), NeurIPS, arXiv 2307.12868.** Verified. Pullback-metric analysis of x_t to h-space; the local latent basis changes across timesteps (coarse to fine).
- **Direct timestep probes in image diffusion.** Only one found: Lu (2026), arXiv 2605.06169, Appendix H, read directly from the arXiv HTML. A 400-layer rectified-flow DiT with no AdaLN and no timestep embedding; ridge probes on the image-token mean predict t with near-perfect R² across depth; the text-token mean becomes predictive after a few layers (the model routes time information there); and adding hidden-state features removes nearly all residual error left by scalar input statistics. This is the experiment's clock-off arm, done at scale, with the input-statistics control. Single-author preprint, not peer reviewed. I found no paper that probes timestep inside a clock-on image U-Net (absence from two searches; medium confidence).
- **Bottom line for Q2.** The probe method is standard. Position is expected to be near-perfectly decodable in both clock-on (it is injected) and clock-off (it is inferred) models, so a raw R² is not informative. The informative quantities are the margin over the input-statistics baseline and causal tests.

## Q3. Exposure bias and the train–test gap

- **Ning, Sangineto, Porrello, Calderara, Cucchiara (2023), ICML, arXiv 2301.11706.** Verified. Prediction errors accumulate along the sampling chain (the diffusion analog of exposure bias). Perturbing training inputs to simulate this improves FID (CelebA 64: 1.27).
- **Ning, Li, Su, Salah, Ertugrul (2024), ICLR, arXiv 2308.15321.** Verified. Analytic model of the sampling distribution; sampled x_t have larger norm/variance than training x_t at the same t. "Epsilon Scaling", shrinking the network's predicted noise, pulls the trajectory back; works across ADM, DDIM, EDM, LDM, DiT, PFGM++.
- **Li, Qu, Yao, Sun, Moens (2024), ICLR, arXiv 2305.15583.** Verified from the PDF text. At each step the Time-Shift Sampler measures the variance of the current sample x_{t-1} and picks, within a window around the nominal step, the timestep whose schedule variance (1 − ᾱ) is closest. That is: an external one-number noise-level estimate, fed back as the label, improves samples. Two implications: a single input statistic already locates the state well enough to be useful, and the clock-on network does not fully correct for a wrong label from content alone.
- **Yu, Sun, Zeng, Chu, Zhan (2026), CVPR, arXiv 2604.16044.** Verified from the arXiv HTML. Their Fig. 1(b) is close to a displacement experiment at the output: a network with fixed timestep s receives forward-process samples from other t. Lower-SNR inputs give larger noise predictions, higher-SNR inputs smaller ones, and predictions are "significantly inaccurate" under mismatch. So the output follows the content partly and the label partly. They also find sampled states have lower SNR than forward states at the same t.
- **Lin, Liu, Li, Yang (2024), WACV, arXiv 2305.08891.** Verified. SD-class schedules do not reach zero SNR at the last step, so at t = T the "noise" still carries signal (low-frequency brightness), and the model learns to exploit it. Relevant to the SD 1.5 arm: early steps of that model are not pure-noise states, and the true noise level of a sampled state differs from the nominal one by schedule design.
- **Bottom line for Q3.** For a clock-on network, "does it act on actual noise or on the schedule?" has a published output-level answer: both, with measurable error from the label. What has not been measured is how the *internal* position read splits between label and content, and whether that split predicts the output error. That is the defensible novel core of the displacement arm.

## Q4. Ensemble spread and guidance as internal referents

- **Aithal, Maini, Lipton, Kolter (2024), NeurIPS, arXiv 2406.09358.** Verified. Variance of the predicted clean image across the final sampling steps flags hallucinated (out-of-support) samples; a simple threshold removes over 95% of them while keeping 96% of in-support samples (workshop figure). This is a single-run, trajectory-level uncertainty signal, read from outputs, not activations.
- **Berry, Brando, Meger (2024), UAI, DECU.** Verified (PMLR v244). Ensembles of conditional diffusion models sharing a pretrained backbone; pairwise-distance estimators of epistemic uncertainty. This is model-weight uncertainty, not seed spread.
- **Chan, Molina, Metzler (2024), NeurIPS, HyperDM, arXiv 2402.03478.** Verified via search listing. Hyper-network ensemble for epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty. Same caveat.
- **Meng, Song, Li, Ermon (2021), NeurIPS, arXiv 2111.04726.** Verified. Tweedie's formula extended to second order: the posterior covariance of x_0 given x_t can be estimated by denoising, which "quantifies denoising uncertainty". *Inference:* the seed-ensemble spread at step t is, in expectation, this posterior covariance, which a denoiser's Jacobian already encodes. So "does one run encode how undetermined it is?" has a theoretical yes at the level of the function; whether it is linearly readable from mid-block activations is open.
- **Biroli, Bonnaire, de Bortoli, Mézard (2024), Nature Communications, arXiv 2402.18491; Sclocchi, Favero, Wyart (2025), PNAS, arXiv 2402.16991.** Verified. Trajectories commit to coarse structure (class) at a sharp "speciation" time and to details later. Ensemble spread is therefore a non-monotone, step-dependent quantity with a known transition, which gives the spread probe a sharp prediction to test against, and also means spread is strongly confounded with t. The spread probe must control for t (partial out t, or evaluate within a narrow t band).
- **Guidance magnitude from activations.** No work found that probes the classifier-free-guidance disagreement (conditional minus unconditional prediction) from activations. Adjacent: prompt-aware CFG scale prediction from text embeddings (arXiv 2509.22728, not from activations) and a gradient-free classifier guidance method that sets scale from the model's "confusion" (arXiv 2411.15393; mechanism not checked). Both unverified beyond abstracts.
- **Bottom line for Q4.** Both secondary referents look open. The spread probe needs t partialled out, and the CFG probe needs a check that it beats the prompt embedding alone (inputs are the first suspect).

## Q5. Feasibility on a T4

- **Small DDPM in about an hour.** I found no published T4 timing (unverified). Reference points: original DDPM CIFAR-10 runs are hundreds of thousands of iterations on TPUs, far beyond an hour; a single-GPU learning project reports MNIST (9.5M parameters, 50 epochs) FID 34 and CIFAR-10 (46M parameters, 100 epochs) FID 65–71 (search-snippet figures, unverified). Chávez (2026) reports FIDs of 35–70 at CIFAR-10/CelebA scale for both clock-on and clock-off twins, a sign that short training still gives a usable comparison. *Recommendation (inference):* MNIST or Fashion-MNIST at 28×28 (or 32×32 padded), a U-Net of roughly 5–15M parameters, T = 1000 training steps with 50–100 sampling steps, fp16 autocast, batch 128; tens of epochs should fit in an hour. CIFAR-10 32×32 is possible but samples will be poor; the probe question does not need good samples, only a trained denoiser.
- **Two models, not one.** Because the clock-off arm needs a model trained without a clock (see below), budget two trainings of the same architecture (clock-on and clock-off, same seed and data order), ideally a third with clock dropout. That is two to three T4 hours, or one hour with a smaller model.
- **SD 1.5 in fp16 on 15 GB.** The U-Net is about 860M parameters, about 1.7 GB of weights in fp16; inference at 512×512 with diffusers fits comfortably in a T4's 15 GB (common practice, not re-verified here). The mid-block output at 512×512 is 1280 channels × 8 × 8 = 81,920 values per image per step, so caching pooled or full mid-block activations with a forward hook is cheap: 1,000 trajectories × 50 steps × 1,280 pooled values is about 128 MB in fp16. Chen et al.'s public probe code is a working template for hooks on SD. Note the non-zero terminal SNR (Lin et al. 2024) when defining "true noise level" for this arm.

## Q6. Verdict on novelty

- **Clock on: not novel, and close to circular.** The clock is injected, so a probe reading t from mid-block activations reads the input. Keep it only as a calibration of the probe.
- **Clock off: answered.** Behaviourally by Sun et al. (2025) and Chávez (2026); representationally by Lu (2026) for a large blind DiT, including the comparison against input statistics; and for text diffusion by Rulli et al. (2026) and arXiv 2607.15893. A small blind DDPM U-Net will almost certainly carry a linearly readable position. A small replication on a substrate we own is still worth running as the baseline for the other arms, but it should not be registered as the headline question.
- **"Hold the embedding constant or scramble it at inference" is the wrong manipulation.** A clock-on network given a fixed or random label is out of distribution; Yu et al. (2026) and Li et al. (2024) show its outputs go wrong. A probe result from that condition mixes "infers position from content" with "is broken by a lying label". Sun et al. and Chávez both answer the question properly by training without the clock.
- **Displacement: partly novel, if restated.** Since a step is memoryless, the arm is really "label says t, content is at t′". The output-level answer exists (Yu et al. 2026: both matter). The internal decomposition — what fraction of the position read follows the label versus the content, by layer — does not appear in the literature I found. That is the novel core.
- **Sham displacement: novel as a control, cheap, keep it.** Swapping a same-level state from another trajectory should not move the position read. No prior work runs it. Rulli et al.'s norm-matched random steering direction is the nearest analog.
- **Ensemble spread and CFG magnitude probes: apparently open.** Two searches each found adjacent work (trajectory variance, Tweedie second moments, speciation times) but no activation probe. Medium confidence.
- **DDIM inversion positive control.** Not searched in depth (outside the six questions). One remark from Q1: deterministic DDIM is the one sampler that fails badly without a clock (Sun et al. 2025), so the inversion control should use the clock-on model, or should expect the clock-off model to invert poorly.

## Recommended design changes before registration

1. **Add an input-statistics baseline to every probe.** Fit the same probe on simple features of x_t (per-channel variance, mean, a few band-pass energies, a Laplacian-residual norm, and the 30K-parameter CNN estimator of Sun et al. as an upper baseline). A position read counts only when activations beat this baseline on held-out trajectories. This is the program's law "inputs are the first suspect", and Lu (2026) shows how to report it (fraction of residual error removed by adding hidden-state features).
2. **Replace the inference-time clock ablation with trained twins.** Train (a) clock-on, (b) clock-off from scratch, and if time allows (c) clock-dropout (the label is replaced by a null token on, say, 20% of training batches), so "clock off" is an in-distribution condition for the same weights. Report sample quality for each, so a probe result is never read on a broken model.
3. **Recast displacement as a 2×2: content level (t or t′) × label (t or t′).** Use forward-noised states (exact ground-truth level) as well as sampled states (level known only from the schedule; measure it with the variance estimator, as in Li et al.). Fit the internal read as a weighted mix of t and t′, by layer, and test whether that weight predicts the output error that Yu et al. measured. Grade the displacement size: small offsets at the scale of exposure bias, and large ones.
4. **State the pre-registered readings in label-versus-content terms.** "Reads the schedule" can only mean "follows the label"; it cannot occur in the clock-off model, which has no schedule input. Say so in the registration so a clock-off result cannot be read as the network "trusting evidence over expectation".
5. **Keep the sham, and add the label-only sham.** Same-level content from another trajectory with the true label (position read must not move), and true content with the true label re-encoded through a second, equivalent path (e.g. the same t passed twice or from a cached embedding) to check the hook itself does not move the read.
6. **For the spread probe, partial out t.** Fit the probe within narrow t bands, or regress t out of both target and features first. Prediction to pre-register: probe skill peaks near the speciation time (Biroli et al. 2024; Sclocchi et al. 2025). Add the Tweedie second-moment estimate (Meng et al. 2021) as the theoretical baseline the activation probe must match or beat.
7. **For the CFG-magnitude probe, beat the prompt.** Baseline: the same probe on the text embedding alone. Only the excess counts.
8. **Scrambled-geometry and held-out controls stay as in the lane law**, applied to every probe above.
