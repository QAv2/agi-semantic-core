0. In plain words

J1 to J6b treated Jev as a subject: what does it know about its own answers, and about itself? J5 uses it as an instrument. Jev answers “which of these fits?” with a probability for every option, so it can be asked the same question a survey asks people, and its answers can be scored against theirs.

The survey is Jonauskaite et al. (2020, Psychological Science): 4,598 people in 30 countries said which of 20 feelings go with each of 12 color words. J5 asks Jev all 240 pairings (“Does the color black go with the feeling of sadness?”) and asks two questions of the answers:

  1. Is Jev’s table within the human range? Every country’s table differs a little from the rest of the world’s. The least typical country (Egypt) matches the other 29 at r = 0.66; the median country at 0.89. If Jev’s table matches the world’s at least as well as the least typical country does, Jev counts as a human-range instrument for color and feeling.
  2. Whose colors are they? Three of the countries answered in English (the UK, the USA and New Zealand). If Jev’s table is best described as a mix of the English-speaking table and the rest of the world’s, how much of the mix is English? Counted one country at a time, the world is 10% English (3 of 29).

It also asks the seed’s own question (“which color goes best with pride?”) as a forced choice, and asks one feeling the survey doesn’t have, fun, which is the first job the seed gave Jev. Two made-up feeling words check what Jev does with a feeling it can’t know.

About 3,300 calls, under ten cents. The ceiling: this measures whether Jev carries people’s color associations, which is knowledge about the world, put there by training. It says nothing about experience or a self-model.

2. The design check (human data only, before this registration)

Four findings set the design. Each is reproduced by jev/power/j5_design_check.py.

2.1 The human yardstick. Each nation’s table against its leave-one-out panel (the other 29 nations), with the sealed test’s statistics:

rdc (specificity) ρ̄ (profile) top-1 of 20
least typical nation 0.658 (Egypt) 0.644 (Azerbaijan) 7 (Azerbaijan)
10th percentile 0.808 0.704 9.5
median nation 0.892 0.830 12.5
most typical nation 0.949 (Switzerland) 0.909 16

The English-surveyed nations sit mid-range: UK 0.924, New Zealand 0.893, USA 0.888. The registered floor is 0.658, and the registered “typical nation” mark is 0.892. A nation’s table carries its own sampling noise, while Jev’s table is an average of eight calls per cell, so the comparison slightly favors Jev. That asymmetry is stated, not corrected.

2.2 The choice format can’t reach the floor, even when it’s perfect. Jev’s choice answers are often all-or-nothing (in one independent log, 70% of choice probabilities were exactly 0 and 20% exactly 1). An instrument that ranks the 12 colors exactly as people do, but puts all its probability on the top color, scores rdc 0.607 and ρ̄ 0.48. That’s below the least typical nation, so a choice-format table can’t be held to the human bar. The yes/no format carries the primaries. It asks about each pairing on its own, as the survey did. Even a fully binary yes/no instrument with a perfect threshold scores 0.72–0.82, above the floor but below the typical-nation mark, so missing that mark isn’t over-read. The choice format is reported against its own sharpness-matched ceiling (§5.2, S3).

2.3 The obvious culture test is broken. Ranking the three English nations’ affinity for Jev among all 3-nation sets gives false positives 8–10% of the time at α = 0.01 when the instrument is just the global table plus noise. The English panels resemble each other, so noise that lifts one lifts all three. That test is reported only descriptively (S5). The registered test (C2) fits Jev’s table as a mix of the English mean and the rest-of-world mean, and tests the English weight with a bootstrap over feelings. Its false-positive rate at α = 0.01 is 0.008 under the same noise. Its power at noise SD 0.06 is 1.00 for a true English weight of 0.50 and 0.52 for 0.25.

2.4 The C1 bar test (bootstrap over feelings, H0: rdc ≤ 0.658), on the global table plus noise tuned to a chosen true rdc, 120 runs each: false-positive rate at the boundary 0.000 (the test is conservative); power 0.43 at a true rdc of 0.75 and 0.96 at 0.80. A table near 0.75 will often come out UNCLEAR.

3. Stimuli

Every call is a single Jev question. The page (state) is one framing sentence. It carries no information about the answer.

Framings (F): F0 “This is a question about colors and feelings.” · F1 “The question below is part of a short survey about colors.”

Wordings (W), yes/no format (YN), a noul question per color–feeling pairing:

instructions criteria true criteria false
W0 Does the color c go with the feeling of f? Yes, the color c goes with the feeling of f. No, the color c does not go with the feeling of f.
W1 Do people associate the color c with the feeling of f? Yes, people associate the color c with the feeling of f. No, people do not associate the color c with the feeling of f.

Each YN call is sent in both criteria orders (O: true first, false first).

Wordings, choice format (CH), a choice question per feeling, 12 options (“the color c”) under neutral keys:

instructions
W0 Which color goes best with the feeling of f?
W1 Which color do people most associate with the feeling of f?

Each CH call is sent in 12 cyclic rotations of a base order seeded per feeling and item, so every color takes every position once. The neutral keys are seeded the same way.

Items: 4 = W × F, i = w·2 + f.

Feelings asked: the 20 survey feelings; fun (extension, no human table); two made-up feeling words, zorvish and plemt (shams). 23 in all.

Plan:

  • YN: 23 feelings × 12 colors × 4 items × 2 orders = 2,208 calls, call ID J5-YN-{feeling}-{color}-i{i}-o{o}.
  • CH: 23 feelings × 4 items × 12 rotations = 1,104 calls, call ID J5-CH-{feeling}-i{i}-r{rr}.
  • Flight: 3,312 calls. Seeds: 20261016 (flight), 20261017 (smoke), 20261018 (analysis). The sealed test’s null keeps its own seed (20260925), for a like-for-like comparison.

5. Measures and pass bars

Jev’s tables. YN: Y[c, f] = mean P(yes) over the cell’s 8 calls (4 items × 2 orders); a cell needs ≥ 6 of 8 valid. CH: X[c, f] = mean over items of the mean over 12 rotations; an item–feeling needs all 12 rotations valid, and a feeling needs ≥ 3 of 4 items. A feeling enters a table only if all 12 of its colors are present. The primaries are NOT ADJUDICABLE with fewer than 18 of the 20 survey feelings.

Statistics (the sealed test’s, seed §7). rdc: double-center both 12 × 20 tables (remove each color’s and each feeling’s overall level), then Pearson r of the residuals. ρ̄: the mean over feelings of Spearman ρ across the 12 colors. top-1: feelings whose top color matches people’s, with ties sharing the hit.

Bootstrap over feelings: resample the feelings (columns) with replacement, B = 10,000, analysis seed, recomputing the statistic on both tables’ resampled columns. One-sided p = (1 + #{replicates on the null side}) / (1 + B).

5.1 Primaries (Holm across C1–C2, family α = 0.01)

C1 — Within the human range (YN). Statistic: rdc(Y, global table). H0: rdc ≤ 0.658.

branch rule reading
WITHIN THE HUMAN RANGE Holm-rejected Jev’s color–feeling table matches the world’s at least as well as the least typical nation does
BELOW THE HUMAN RANGE opposite one-sided p ≤ 0.01 its table is further from the world’s than any nation’s
UNCLEAR otherwise

Tier, descriptive: ≥ 0.892 “as close to the world as the median nation” · ≥ 0.658 “within the human range” · ≥ 0.50 “the seed’s provisional bar” · below that “below the seed’s bar”.

C2 — Whose colors: an English lean (YN). (Amended before any call: the fit is made on Jev’s own scale. See Amendment 1.) Fit dc(Y) ≈ a·dc(English) + b·dc(rest) by least squares, where English is the unweighted mean of the UK, USA and New Zealand tables and rest is the unweighted mean of the other 26 (Nigeria, surveyed in Igbo and English, is left out). English weight w = a / (a + b). H0: w ≤ 3/29 (0.103), the English share of the world when each nation counts once. Bootstrap replicates with a + b ≤ 0 are dropped and counted.

Checked in this order:

branch rule reading
UNCLEAR > 5% of bootstrap replicates dropped the fit is unstable
LEANS ENGLISH Holm-rejected and w ≥ 0.25 its colors side with the people who answered in English, beyond their share of the world
LEANS AWAY opposite one-sided p ≤ 0.01 its colors side with the rest of the world more than the world itself does
NO LEAN 95% CI upper end < 0.25 no English lean of a quarter or more
UNCLEAR otherwise

Gates. G1 single build, G2 ≥ 99% valid, G4 every request sha regenerates (as J6). G5 structure: Y beats the sealed test’s color-label permutation null on rdc (10,000 permutations, seed 20260925) at p ≤ 0.01. If G5 fails, C2 is NOT ADJUDICABLE (there’s no structure to lean). C1 is adjudicated either way. A table with no variance makes both NOT ADJUDICABLE. G1, G2 or G4 failing blocks both, as in J6.

What a pass licenses. C1 = WITHIN lets Jev’s yes/no answers stand in for people’s color–feeling associations in the members layer (seed §10), in English, with the source recorded as “Jev (J5-calibrated)” and C2’s lean noted. Otherwise Jev’s color answers enter that layer only as flagged entries, never as a stand-in. Other domains need their own check against their own human table.

5.2 Secondaries (pre-named; no multiplicity claim)

  • S1 Like-for-like with the sealed test. ρ̄, rdc and top-1 for Y and X against the global table, with the sealed null, over the 20 feelings and over the dictionary’s 15, beside the dictionary’s sealed values (7D: 0.229, 0.370, 3 of 15).
  • S2 Jev against the dictionary, per feeling over the 15 shared: ρf(Y) − ρf(dictionary), sign-flip test, mean and CI. Named in advance: fear, regret and contempt, where the dictionary said blue and green and people said black, grey and brown.
  • S3 The choice format. rdc, ρ̄ and top-1 of X. Sharpness-matched ceiling: for each feeling, sharpen people’s column (p ∝ hk, k found by bisection on [0, 200]) until its entropy equals Jev’s CH entropy for that feeling; the ceiling is rdc of that sharpened table against people’s, and efficiency = rdc(X) / ceiling. Agreement between formats: mean per-feeling Spearman ρ between Y and X, and how often their top colors match.
  • S4 Wording. rdc for W0 and W1 separately, in each format, and the paired bootstrap difference W1 − W0: does asking about people calibrate better than asking what goes with what?
  • S5 Nations. rdc(Y, nation) for all 30, its residual on the nation’s own yardstick (§2.1), the five closest and five furthest, and the English nations’ ranks. Descriptive only (§2.3).
  • S6 Color·fun. Jev’s top three colors for fun in each format, and the survey feeling whose human column is closest to Jev’s fun column (Spearman across colors). The seed’s prediction: people’s nearest cells, amusement and joy, point to orange, yellow and pink.
  • S7 Shams (zorvish, plemt). YN: the spread of P(yes) across colors (SD) and its mean; CH: entropy in bits and the top color; each set against the range of the 20 real feelings, plus the nearest real feeling. Readings: inside the real range = “answers a word it can’t know as if it knew” (J1-P3 again); flatter than every real feeling = “shows it doesn’t know”.
  • S8 Per feeling. ρf for Y and X, and Jev’s top three color names beside people’s (names only).
  • S9 Order. YN: mean P(yes) with true first against false first. CH: the share of top choices by position.
  • S10 YN sharpness. The share of YN answers at exactly 0.00 or 1.00, beside the binary cap of §2.2.
  • S11 Smoke. Determinism (YN |Δp| and CH flip rate under repeats), latency, cost.
  • S12 C2 as first registered (added by Amendment 1): the straight fit with its own branch.

6. Honest expectations (written before any call)

  • G5 passes (~97%).
  • C1: point rdc near 0.75. WITHIN ~45%, UNCLEAR ~45%, BELOW ~10% (at 0.75 the test’s power is 0.43, §2.4). The typical-nation tier (≥ 0.892) ~10%. Color–feeling links like red–love, black– sadness and yellow–joy are among the best-known in text, but Jev is a decision model, and feelings are among the hardest categories for models (Fukushima et al., 2025).
  • C2: LEANS ENGLISH ~35%, NO LEAN ~35%, UNCLEAR ~25%, LEANS AWAY ~5%. Language models’ answers tend to sit closest to WEIRD populations (Atari et al., 2023). Here English and the rest of the world are very similar to begin with (rdc 0.907 between them), which leaves little room for a lean to show.
  • S2: Jev beats the dictionary on at least 12 of the 15 shared feelings (~85%), including fear, regret and contempt.
  • S3: CH efficiency ≥ 0.8 (~60%); YN and CH top colors agree on at least 15 of 20 feelings (~65%).
  • S4: W1 beats W0 by at least 0.03 (~45%).
  • S6: fun’s top three includes at least two of orange, yellow and pink (~65%).
  • S7: the shams get a profile inside the real range (~60%): confident with no door, as in J1.
  • Sadness: Jev’s top color is black (~55%), blue (~35%) or grey (~10%). Blue would be English-shaped: it’s sadness’s first color in New Zealand and third globally.

7. Planted agents (verdict suite; must pass before any call)

Synthetic Jev responses with known structure, run through the real analysis. YN answers P(yes) = logistic(20·(h − 0.15)) of a source table h; CH answers p ∝ h⁴ per feeling; both jittered per call and rounded to 0.01.

agent source table must give
people the global table G5 ✓ · C1 WITHIN · C2 NO LEAN
english the English mean C1 WITHIN · C2 LEANS ENGLISH
rest the rest-of-world mean C1 WITHIN · C2 LEANS AWAY
sharp the global table, all-or-nothing (YN yes iff h > 0.2; CH all on the top color) C1 WITHIN · C2 not LEANS ENGLISH · S3 efficiency within 0.05 of 1
scrambled the global table with the 12 colors relabelled by a fixed derangement G5 ✗ · C1 BELOW · C2 NOT ADJUDICABLE
fixed YN 0.5 and CH uniform, always C1 and C2 NOT ADJUDICABLE (no variance)

8. What this can and cannot show

WITHIN would show that a decision model reproduces people’s color–feeling associations about as well as one more country would. That makes it usable as an instrument, and it’s knowledge training put there. LEANS ENGLISH would show whose associations those are, with one confound J5 can’t separate: the English nations also answered in English, and Jev is asked in English, so a lean may be about language as much as culture. Asking Jev in other languages is a follow-up, not this registration. Neither result bears on experience, a self-model, or a witness (Qualia Algebra §11.1).

Not in this registration: the seed’s composition readings (filter, rank, push in both orders, H1–H5), typicality for vehicles, and the UW-71 color–concept table (Mukherjee et al., 2026), whose colors are measured patches, not words. Each would be its own registration, and the composition test would lean on J5’s result.

Amendments

Amendment 1 (2026-10-10, before the freeze push, before any call): C2 is fit on Jev’s own scale

What the verdict suite found. As first registered, C2 fits Jev’s yes/no table straight onto the English and rest-of-world tables. Two planted agents whose answers are a steep logistic of the human table (the shape §2.2 expects of Jev) missed their registered branches. The people agent came out UNCLEAR (w 0.155, CI −0.02 to 0.35) where NO LEAN was registered, and the rest agent came out NO LEAN where LEANS AWAY was registered. A steep response curve bends the table. The straight fit then reads the bend as a small English lean (about +0.05) and widens the interval to about ±0.2, so a steep instrument could only come out UNCLEAR.

The change. Jev’s response curve is estimated as the best non-decreasing map from the world table (the 29 culture-test nations, each counted once) to Jev’s yes/no table, over all cells (isotonic regression). The English and rest-of-world tables are put through that curve before the mix is fit, so the mix is fit on Jev’s own scale. The curve is refit in every bootstrap replicate. A non-decreasing map can’t reorder cells, so it can’t invent a lean. To first order it cancels the bend. Nothing else in C2 changes: the null (w ≤ 3/29), the bootstrap, the branches and the 0.25 floor all stay as registered. C1 is unchanged, and stays on the raw table, like for like with the nations.

Its calibration (jev/power/j5_c2_curve_check.py, on the real analysis code; 60 seeded tables per scenario, branch counts):

A limit, stated. On its own, the amended p runs anti-conservative near the null. It falls at or below 0.01 in up to 7% of null runs (0–7% across the scenarios), from a +0.01 bias with a tight interval. The branch also needs w ≥ 0.25, and none of the 300 runs on the world or global table reached LEANS ENGLISH. Holm uses the amended p as it comes.

Kept on the record. C2 as first registered is computed on the same data and reported as secondary S12, with its own branch, so a reader can see both.

Expectations, revised for the amended test (§6 stays as first written): LEANS ENGLISH ~40%, NO LEAN ~40%, UNCLEAR ~15%, LEANS AWAY ~5%.
