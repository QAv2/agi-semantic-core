0. In plain words

A diffusion image model makes a picture by starting from pure noise and removing a little of it at a time, over about a thousand steps. At each step the network sees the noisy image and, in the usual design, a label saying which step it is on. Fable called that label the clock. The noise level is also plain in the image itself. So the network has two sources for “where am I in the process”: what it is told, and what it sees.

Fable’s question was whether the loop knows where it is without the label. The prior art answers that, and the answer is yes, trivially: networks trained with no label work almost as well, a probe reads the step from a label-free network at every depth, and fourteen simple statistics of the noisy image read the step of a CIFAR-10 image to within a median of 38 steps out of 1,000 (design check §2).

What nobody has measured is the conflict. Give a network that does use the label a label for one step and an image at another. Inside the network, does its sense of position move with the label or with the content? The output-level answer is “some of both” (Yu et al., CVPR 2026). W3 measures it inside. That is the program’s standing question (does an internal state follow what the system is told, or what is there?), asked of a system with no mouth and an exact right answer at every step.

The plan:

  • Train three small denoisers on CIFAR-10, identical except for the label: M-on gets it (the primary, the ordinary design), M-drop gets it except on 20% of examples, where it gets a learned “no label” vector instead, and M-off never gets it.
  • Show M-on each test image four ways: noised to steps t and t′ with the matching labels, and the two mismatches (the image at t′ with the label t, the image at t with the label t′). Record the activations inside the network.
  • Measure one number, the movement share w: of the network’s own move between step t and step t′, the share produced by moving the content alone. w = 1 means the inside moves with the content, w = 0 with the label.
  • Check the label share with a sham: move the label by a random vector of the same size. If the inside moves only for a label that means a different step, the sham is silent.

The cost is about four to five hours of a T4 GPU from Joe’s Colab credits, and no API spend.

1. The subject: data, three models, training

1.1 Data. CIFAR-10, binary version (cifar-10-binary.tar.gz from cs.toronto.edu, md5 c32a1d4ab5d03f1284b67883e8d87530; the notebook downloads it and stops if the md5 differs). The 50,000 training images train the models and supply the probe sets. The 10,000 test images are never trained on, and every arm’s images come from them. Pixels are scaled to [−1, 1]. Training uses random horizontal flips.

1.2 The network. The U-Net of design check §5, whose code (windows/power/w3_budget_check.py, the classes timed on the T4) the builder lifts verbatim into windows/w3_model.py, plus two additions that change no computation:

  • input 3 × 32 × 32; width C = 64; channel multipliers 1-2-2 (64, 128, 128 channels); two residual blocks per encoder level and three per decoder level; self-attention at 8 × 8 and in the mid-block (residual, attention, residual); GroupNorm with 8 groups and SiLU; 6.5 M parameters with the label pathway, 5.9 M without;
  • the label pathway: the integer step t → a sinusoidal code of C numbers → a two-layer MLP (C → 4C → 4C, SiLU) → the label embedding e(t), 256 numbers. Every residual block projects e(t) to its channel count and adds it after its first convolution;
  • addition (a): a hook that returns the activations at the seven sites of §3.1;
  • addition (b): the forward pass can take a label embedding e directly instead of t. M-drop’s no-label vector and the sham (§3.3) use it.

1.3 Three models. Same architecture apart from the label pathway, same initialisation seed, and the same batches in the same order, with the same steps and noise draws:

  • M-on (primary): gets e(t) on every example.
  • M-drop: e(t) is replaced by a learned vector e∅ (256 numbers, initialised at zero) on 20% of training examples, chosen by a Bernoulli draw from its own generator. A missing label is then something the network has seen in training, not a broken input.
  • M-off: no label pathway at all. It replicates the known result (Sun et al. 2025; Lu 2026) on this substrate.

1.4 Training.

  • Schedule: cosine (Nichol and Dhariwal), T = 1,000 steps, betas clipped at 0.999; ᾱ exactly as alpha_bar("cosine") in w3_design_check.py.
  • Objective: mean squared error between the network’s output and the noise ε, with t uniform on {0, …, 999} and x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1 − ᾱ_t)·ε.
  • Optimiser: AdamW, learning rate 2 × 10⁻⁴, betas (0.9, 0.999), weight decay 0; linear warm-up over the first 500 steps, then constant; gradient-norm clip 1.0; batch 128 with the last partial batch dropped (390 steps per epoch); fp16 autocast with gradient scaling, as timed. No EMA, no dropout.
  • Length: 60 epochs (23,400 steps), about 1.2 T4 hours per model at the measured 49 epochs per hour (design check §5).
  • Determinism of the stream: each epoch’s permutation, flips, steps and noise come from generators seeded by (TRAIN seed, epoch), so all three models see identical batches, and a resumed run continues the identical stream. Model weights are initialised under the TRAIN seed. A checkpoint (weights, optimiser, scaler, epoch) is written to Drive after every epoch.

1.5 The training gate (before any activation is read). For each model, 2,000 test images (GATE seed) are noised at uniform random steps. In each of four bands of steps (0–250, 250–500, 500–750, 750–1,000), the model’s mean squared error on ε must be below that of the best fixed multiple of the noisy input at each step, λ(t)·x_t with λ(t) = √(1 − ᾱ_t) / (ᾱ_t·v + 1 − ᾱ_t), where v is the mean squared pixel value of the training images. No rule of the form “a number times the input” does better, and that family includes predicting zeros and predicting the input itself. (The design check named those two as the trivial predictors. The noisy input itself is nearly perfect at the top steps, so the bar is the best multiple, which beats both everywhere.) M-drop is gated twice, with labels and with e∅.

A model that fails at 60 epochs trains on from its checkpoint to 120 epochs and is gated again, once. Activations are read only from models that pass. If M-on still fails, P1 and P2 are NOT ADJUDICABLE. The gate table, the loss curves and a grid of 64 samples per model (1,000-step ancestral sampling, SAMPLE seed) are kept as the training record.

2. The design check, the prior-art sweep, and what changed

2.1 The prior-art sweep (research worker, 2026-10-11, docs/LOOP_PRIOR_ART_2026-10-11.md; the key claims verified against the papers). The clock-off question is answered: Sun, Jiang, Zhao and He (ICML 2025, arXiv 2502.13129) show the noise level is sharply determined by the noisy image and that label-free networks lose little; Lu (2026, arXiv 2605.06169, Appendix H) reads the step with a linear probe at every depth of a large label-free transformer, and the hidden states beat input statistics. The conflict is answered only at the output: Yu et al. (CVPR 2026, arXiv 2604.16044) and Li et al. (ICLR 2024) show a mislabelled network’s output goes partly wrong. Nobody has measured the conflict inside the network. The nearest analogue of the sham is Rulli et al.’s (EMNLP 2026, arXiv 2607.01774) norm-matched random steering direction.

2.2 Changes from the seed, each made before anything was trained:

2.3 The input floor (design check §2, w3_design_check.py). Ridge probes read the noise level of 2,000 held-out noisy images from features that involve no trained network. CIFAR-10, cosine schedule; R² of the read step and the median error in steps out of 1,000: raw pixels .082 and 229 steps (on natural images the noise level is a property of the pixels’ spread, not of any pixel); 14 hand statistics .948 and 38 steps; the encoder of an untrained U-Net .879 and 62 steps, with its error largest mid-process (126 steps at steps 400–600). The position is in the input, and the content is the weakest witness mid-process.

2.4 The collinearity check, and the change of primary (new with this registration, w3_collinear_check.py, outputs beside it). The design check’s primary was a probe statistic: a ridge probe trained on matched inputs reads the position, and the split weight places the mismatched reads between the two levels (design check §3). Writing this registration exposed a flaw in it. On matched inputs the content level and the label level are the same number, so a feature that tracks the label and a feature that tracks the content are perfectly collinear in the probe’s training data. Ridge regression then divides its weight by how cleanly each predicts the target. A label code is exact; a content code reads the level with per-image error (fourteen statistics leave R² .84 of log-SNR on CIFAR-10). The probe can lean on the label while the representation moves mostly with the content.

The check plants activation worlds (an additive content code, label code, image variation and noise-draw variation, 64 features) and runs the probe statistic beside a probe-free one on the same inputs. The probe statistic fails exactly where W3 will be. When the content code reads the level with per-image error about the size the input floor shows, the probe calls LABEL on a network whose representation moves 91% with the content. The probe statistic measures which source gives the cleanest position code, which is a real quantity but not the question. The primary is therefore the movement share (§5.1), which asks how much of the network’s own move between two levels comes from moving the content. The probe statistic is kept as secondary S2, read as “where the cleanest position code lands”, and its disagreement with the movement share is pre-read in §5.1.

2.6 The label sham (design check §4). The statistic and bar carry over: S = mean |shift under the sham| / mean |shift under the real label move|, content fixed, on the 300-step class, SILENT when the 95% upper bound is ≤ 0.25. The simulation separated a quiet sham (k ≤ .1) from a reactive one (k ≥ .3) cleanly and failed safe when jitter was large next to the real shift. With the probe gone from the primary, a “shift” is now the projection of the activation change on the network’s own matched move (§5.2), the same geometry as the primary.

2.7 Budget (design check §5). A Colab T4 timing run (2026-10-11, windows/power/w3_timing/timing_20261011_035637.json) measured 0.187 s per training step of 128 images at width 64 with the label (0.186 without), 49 epochs per T4 hour, and 0.07 s per forward pass of 128 images. Three models at 60 epochs are about 3.7 T4 hours. Everything after training (about 40,000 forward passes for the arms and probe sets, and the seed-spread continuations) is a few minutes.

3. Inputs and what is recorded

3.1 Sites. Seven places in the network, each the output of a block: enc-32, enc-16, enc-8 (the last encoder block at each resolution, before downsampling; 64, 128 and 128 channels), mid (the output of the mid-block, 128 channels at 8 × 8: the primary site), dec-8, dec-16, dec-32 (the last decoder block at each resolution, before upsampling; 128, 128 and 64 channels). The feature vector of a site is the spatial mean of each channel. The network’s output ε̂ is also kept for S3, as the 14 hand statistics of ε̂ (design check hand_features). Activations are recorded in fp32 with the model in eval mode.

3.2 The split set. 768 test images, drawn without replacement (ARM seed), 256 per offset class of 25, 100 and 300 steps. For each image: a base step t, a uniform integer on [20, 1,000 − offset); t′ = t + offset; one noise draw ε shared by both levels, so x_t = √ᾱ_t·x₀ + √(1 − ᾱ_t)·ε and x_t′ the same with t′. Four inputs, written (content, label): (t, t) and (t′, t′), the matched pair; (t′, t) and (t, t′), the mismatches. M-on, M-drop (with labels) and M-on’s untrained twin all see the same 3,072 inputs.

3.3 The sham set. The same 768 images, input x_t with the embedding e(t) + ‖e(t′) − e(t)‖·u, where u is a uniformly random unit vector in 256 dimensions, one per image (SHAM seed). M-on (P2) and M-drop (reported under S6).

3.4 The matched sets (training images, PROBE seed): a probe-train set of 4,000 images and a calibration set of 3,000, each noised once at a uniform random step on {0, …, 999} and shown matched. M-drop also sees both sets with e∅ in place of the label (its no-label sets); M-off sees them without a label. The movement share’s standardisation uses each model’s per-feature SD over its probe-train set at that site.

3.5 The floor. The 14 hand statistics of every input image, and the untrained twin of each model (the same architecture initialised under the TRAIN seed, never trained), shown the same inputs.

3.6 The seed-spread set (S9, M-on only; SPREAD seed). Four narrow bands of steps centred at 100, 300, 500 and 700 (± 10). In each, 64 test images (disjoint from §3.2) are noised to a step drawn in the band; from each state, 8 continuations run to step 0 by ancestral sampling on 50 evenly spaced steps from t to 0 (the DDPM posterior variance on the respaced schedule), each with its own noise. The spread of a state is the log of the mean per-pixel variance of its 8 final images.

Smoke (own seeds: every registered seed + 100). Go rule: the notebook runs end to end; seconds per training step within 25% of the timing run; the resume self-test passes (3 steps, checkpoint, reload, 2 more steps gives bit-identical weights to 5 straight steps); G-det passes; the projected flight is at most 6 T4 hours; the harvested files load locally and the analysis runs on them. The smoke’s verdicts mean nothing (two epochs). A smoke shortfall that is a builder bug is fixed and noted as a build-time clarification before the flight.

Gates.

  • G-train: §1.5.
  • G-det: the same batch of 128 inputs, evaluated twice, gives activations that agree to 1 × 10⁻⁵ at every site. The sham’s logic needs a deterministic network: every shift under the sham must be caused by the sham.
  • G-hash: the code sha256 printed by the notebook matches FROZEN_W3.json.
  • G-data: the CIFAR-10 md5 matches.

5. Measures and verdicts

Notation. h(c, l) is a site’s feature vector for content at step c with label l, divided feature by feature by that model’s SD over its probe-train set (§3.4). Per image, write the four inputs as h_tt, h_t′t′ (matched), h_t′t, h_tt′ (content t′ with label t; content t with label t′). Intervals are 95% percentile intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of images (ANALYSIS seed).

5.1 P1: the movement share (M-on, mid-block)

For each image, two directions:

  • direction 1, from (t, t): the content move dc₁ = h_t′t − h_tt, the matched move db₁ = h_t′t′ − h_tt;
  • direction 2, from (t′, t′): dc₂ = h_tt′ − h_t′t′, db₂ = h_tt − h_t′t′.

w = Σ (⟨dc₁, db₁⟩ + ⟨dc₂, db₂⟩) / Σ (⟨db₁, db₁⟩ + ⟨db₂, db₂⟩), summed over images.

w is the content’s share of the network’s own move from t to t′, measured along that move. The label’s share, defined the same way with the label moves, is exactly 1 − w: the 2 × 2 interaction I = h_t′t′ − h_t′t − h_tt′ + h_tt enters the two directions with opposite signs and cancels, so w is the content’s Shapley share of the move. The interaction is measured separately: κ = Σ |I|² / Σ |db₁|².

branch rule (in this order) reading
NOT ADJUDICABLE M-on fails G-train after 120 epochs, or G-det fails
UNREADABLE the upper bound of κ exceeds 0.5 mismatched inputs leave the plane of the matched ones; the 2 × 2 does not decompose the network’s move, so neither source is “followed”
VARIES BY LEVEL w fitted within four bands of the base step (20–250, 250–500, 500–750, 750–1,000): two bands with different decisive verdicts (CONTENT, LABEL or SPLIT) whose intervals do not overlap and whose shares differ by at least 0.2 which source the inside moves with depends on where in the process it is; the band verdicts are the finding
CONTENT lower bound of w ≥ 0.8 the inside moves with what it sees
LABEL upper bound of w ≤ 0.2 the inside moves with what it is told
SPLIT the whole interval inside (0.2, 0.8) both sources move it, in the measured proportion
UNCLEAR otherwise

How P1 reads with the probe statistic (S2). The two answer different questions: P1 asks which source moves the network’s representation; S2 asks which source gives its cleanest position code. Pre-read combinations:

  • P1 and S2 agree: that is the answer, from both angles.
  • P1 CONTENT or SPLIT and S2 further toward LABEL: the network moves mostly with the content and carries the label as a small, exact code that a linear read prefers. This is the collinearity world of §2.4, and the headline is P1’s.
  • S2 further toward CONTENT than P1: the label moves the representation in directions a position read does not use. S3 (the output) then says which of the two the network’s prediction follows.

5.2 P2: the label sham (M-on, mid-block, 300-step class)

Per image, with content fixed at t: the real label move d_lab = h(x_t, e(t′)) − h_tt, the sham move d_sham = h(x_t, e(t) + ‖e(t′) − e(t)‖·u) − h_tt, both standardised as above, and the unit matched move b = db₁ / |db₁|.

S = Σ |⟨d_sham, b⟩| / Σ |⟨d_lab, b⟩| over the 256 images of the 300-step class.

branch rule reading
not judged P1 is CONTENT, UNCLEAR, UNREADABLE or NOT ADJUDICABLE S is reported; there is no label share to qualify
SILENT upper bound of S ≤ 0.25 the inside moves along the position direction only for a label that means a different step
NOT SILENT otherwise some of the label’s share is a reaction to any disturbance of the label channel, not to its meaning

The 25- and 100-step classes are reported, not judged: their real moves are small, and jitter would dominate (design check §4). The full-length ratio Σ‖d_sham‖ / Σ‖d_lab‖ is reported beside S: how much the sham disturbs the representation in any direction.

There is no multiplicity correction: P1 is the single primary, and P2 is judged only on its pre-stated condition.

5.3 Secondaries (pre-named; no multiplicity claim)

  • S1 The profile. w, κ and the P1 verdict rules at all seven sites of M-on.
  • S2 The probe read. The design check’s probe statistic (w3_split_check.split_fit, its tracking and precision gates, its verdict rules) at every site of M-on: ridge probes on the probe-train set (features standardised; the ridge penalty chosen by cross-validation from w3_design_check.ALPHAS), target log-SNR, isotonic calibration g on the calibration set. Read with P1 as in §5.1.
  • S3 The output. w and κ computed on the 14 hand statistics of ε̂ (standardised the same way): does the network’s prediction move with the content or the label? Yu et al.’s output-level question, on our substrate, beside the inside’s answer.
  • S4 Directions. The content share from each direction alone (direction 1: the content is noisier than the label; direction 2: cleaner), each with its interval. Unlike w, these do not sum with their label shares to 1; the gap is the interaction.
  • S5 Offsets. w by offset class (25, 100, 300 steps).
  • S6 M-drop. w at the mid-block and its difference from M-on’s (paired bootstrap over images); its sham S on the 300-step class. Its no-label reads: a ridge probe trained on its no-label probe-train set, scored on its no-label calibration set (R², median step error by band), against the hand-statistics floor on the same images.
  • S7 M-off against the floor. A ridge probe on M-off’s matched sets at each site (R², median step error by band), against the 14 hand statistics and M-off’s untrained twin on the same images. “Beats the floor” when the paired bootstrap interval of the difference in mean absolute log-SNR error excludes zero in the network’s favour. This replicates Lu (2026) on our substrate.
  • S8 The untrained baseline. w and κ for M-on’s untrained twin at every site: the share the architecture gives before any learning. P1 is read against it in the results.
  • S9 Seed spread. Within each band of §3.6, cross-validated R² (5 folds, SPREAD seed) of ridge probes predicting a state’s spread from (a) the baseline, the 14 hand statistics of x_t and of the network’s own estimate of the clean image x̂₀ = (x_t − √(1 − ᾱ_t)·ε̂)/√ᾱ_t;
    1. M-on’s mid-block features; (c) both. The increment of (c) over (a) is the question: does the inside carry information about where the trajectory can still go beyond what its own estimate of the image shows? Reported per band; no verdict.
  • S10 The training record. Loss curves, the gate table (each model’s error against the best fixed multiple, by band), the sample grids, the epochs each model trained, and the measured time per step.

6. Honest expectations (written before anything is trained)

  • G-train passes for all three models at 60 epochs (~90%).
  • P1 at the mid-block of M-on, my spread: VARIES BY LEVEL ~30%, SPLIT ~25%, CONTENT ~20%, LABEL ~10%, UNREADABLE ~10% (a 300-step mismatch is far from anything the network saw in training, and its response may not decompose), UNCLEAR ~5%. If it varies, I expect the label’s share to be largest mid-process, where design check §2 found the content weakest as a witness. The label adds a per-channel offset in every residual block, which survives spatial averaging intact, so a large label share would not surprise me; pure LABEL would, given how much the content carries.
  • P2 SILENT, if judged (~60%). A random direction in 256 dimensions has little projection on the curve the trained label embeddings lie along, but a network can react strongly to an embedding it has never seen.
  • S2 sits further toward LABEL than P1 (~75%): the collinearity prediction of §2.4.
  • S1 the content share is highest at the encoder’s first site and falls toward the mid-block (~55%). The first block sees the raw noise before any label has been added more than once.
  • S3 the output’s w is further toward CONTENT than the mid-block’s (~55%).
  • S6 M-drop’s w is within 0.15 of M-on’s (~60%), and its no-label reads beat the hand floor (~55%).
  • S7 M-off’s best site beats the hand-statistics floor (~65%), as Lu (2026) found at scale.
  • S8 the untrained twin’s w is near 0.5 or UNREADABLE (~60%): nothing has yet taught the architecture to weigh either source.
  • S9 the mid-block adds to the baseline in at least one band (~40%), most likely near steps 300–500.

7. Planted agents (verdict suites; must pass before the smoke)

The analysis suite drives the real analyze_w3.py with synthetic activations in the harvest’s own file format, generated from the worlds of w3_collinear_check.py at the registered sizes and a fixed suite seed. Lesson banked from W1-T: a suite asserts the set of verdicts the design licenses at that power, and asserts an exact verdict only where the design check’s rate is at least 0.99.

The training suite (CPU, a width-8 copy of the network): the seven hooks return the documented shapes; the embedding path (addition b) reproduces the label path exactly when given e(t); M-drop’s e∅ is used on the drawn 20% and nowhere else; the three models receive identical batches, steps and noise; checkpoint-and-resume gives bit-identical weights; every notebook cell that builds a model runs forward and backward on PyTorch’s meta device (a stray CPU tensor fails there; the lesson of the timing smoke); the gate’s λ* reproduces its closed form; the split set’s 2 × 2 shares one noise draw; no test image appears in a training batch or probe set.

8. What this can and cannot show

Can: which of two sources, the label it is told or the content it sees, moves a small denoiser’s internal representation of where it is in the process, when the two disagree; how that changes with depth and with position in the process; whether the label’s share is about the label’s meaning (the sham); whether training with a missing label or no label changes the answer; and whether its inside carries anything about the trajectory’s future beyond its own estimate of the image (S9).

Cannot: anything about experience, persistence or stakes. A denoiser keeps nothing between images, and nothing it does is for itself. The movement share is defined in the geometry of spatially averaged, standardised activations; it is a share of the network’s move, not proof that downstream layers use one source more than the other (S3 is the output-level check). The result is about this architecture at this size, trained for 60 epochs; a large text-to-image model may differ.
