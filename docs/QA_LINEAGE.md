## 1. The King's geometry (1443–1446) — history, accurate

Sejong the Great, fourth king of Joseon (r. 1418–1450), completed the
alphabet in the winter of 1443 and promulgated it in 1446 as the
**Hunminjeongeum** (훈민정음, "The Correct Sounds for the Instruction of
the People") — 28 letters. The explanatory edition, the **Hunminjeongeum
Haerye**, sets out the design principles; lost for centuries, a copy
resurfaced in Andong in 1940, and it is the reason we know the geometry
was *deliberate*, not retrofitted.

**Sejong's stated motive** (preface, standard translation): the sounds of
the country's language differ from those of China; among the common
people "there have been many who, having something to put into words,
have in the end been unable to express their feelings. I have been
distressed because of this, and have newly designed twenty-eight letters,
wishing only that everyone may easily learn them and use them
conveniently every day."

**The design is featural** (the term is Geoffrey Sampson's, 1985, coined
*for* Hangul): the graphic atoms encode articulatory features, and
composition rules assemble them.

- **Consonants**: the five basic shapes are drawings of the articulators —
  ㄱ the tongue root blocking the throat, ㄴ the tongue touching the
  ridge, ㅁ the mouth, ㅅ the tooth, ㅇ the open throat — extended by
  stroke-addition for aspiration/tension, and assigned in the Haerye to
  the five phases (오행), seasons, and directions.
- **Vowels**: three primitives with explicit cosmological geometry —
  **• (arae-a): Heaven, round, yang · ㅡ (eu): Earth, flat, yin ·
  ㅣ (i): Human, upright, the mediator standing between them.** Every
  other vowel is a composition of these three (dot above/right of the
  line = yang class; below/left = yin class — vowel harmony is *written
  into the glyphs*).
- **Syllables**: letters compose geometrically into square blocks —
  a two-level algebra (features → letters → syllables) a learner can run
  generatively.

**Jeong Inji's postface** makes the generative claim outright: the wise
can learn the letters before the morning is over, the unwise in ten days
— and "there is no usage not provided for... even the sound of the winds,
the cry of the crane, the cackle of fowl and the barking of dogs — all
may be written." **A small atom set plus a composition rule lets a writer
spell sounds no one ever wrote before.** That sentence is the whole
method of this program, five centuries early.

The scholarly class protested (Choe Manri, 1444) — the logographic
monopoly was a power structure, and a featural alphabet dissolved it.
Literacy for the voiceless was the point.

## 2. Pulled apart — the 90° complementary rule

QA's first structural discovery came from taking Jamo composition apart:
**semantic opposites are complements at ~90°, not antiparallel at 180°.**
Opposition is a *label*; the geometry underneath is orthogonal
complementarity — two poles sharing a space at right angles, the way ㅡ
and ㅣ cross, not the way +x and −x annihilate. This became the immutable
encoding rule of the semantic dictionary (the encoding contract forbids
rotating cores toward 180°), and it has held through every validation
pass since: complement pairs live in the 60–120° band with the canonical
pairs near 90°.

The rule earned an independent echo later, from a source that knew
nothing of the dictionary: Horden's reading of the Penniston sequence —
"things in two different times can occupy the same space" — is complement
geometry said in narrative (hexagram 18, repeated). And LLM embedding
studies (Phase 6) showed the other half: statistical training *compresses*
~95% of complement pairs — the 90° structure is precisely what gradient
descent on co-occurrence does not learn. The rule is the dictionary's
unique cargo.
