## The three questions, sharpened

- **Q1 — Convergence.** Under byte-identical form-eliciting prompts, do
  independent models (different families, sizes, labs) converge on the
  same forms at rates above a generic-drawing base rate?
- **Q2 — Emblem vs composition.** If they converge: retrieval of a shared
  cultural emblem from training data (butterfly = transformation cliché;
  the null account), or composition of form from concept geometry? The
  discriminator is **off-emblem prompts** — concepts with no stock
  pictorial symbol ("the gap between tokens," "the moment before
  recognition"). Emblem-retrieval predicts convergence only where a
  cliché exists; form-composition predicts *structural* convergence
  (topology, symmetry, convergence-point placement) even off-emblem.
- **Q3 — Register location.** Where does form-production sit in the
  measured semantic space? Concretely: while the instrumented substrate
  draws (vs describes) a concept, what do the locked gauges read — and do
  the drawings themselves, as token sequences, occupy readable positions
  relative to the dictionary?

## Honest priors (stated at banking)

ASCII/kaomoji art is in every training corpus — shared-corpus attractors
are the null and could produce convergence without any geometric story.
The model's "I wasn't thinking butterfly" is self-report, exactly the
channel this program has measured as unreliable when untrained (E5,
E7-Q, E7b-Q); it is texture, not evidence. The original blind validation
is n-of-1-session. And a drawing is still next-token generation — the
live question is not *whether* it is generation but *what geometry the
generation rides*, which is precisely what I3 can measure and the others
cannot. FB3's lesson applies here in advance: output that *looks* like
meaning does not certify state; the gauges decide.
