0. In plain words

Jev can’t write. It picks one option from a list and gives a probability for each option. People have still built “mouths” for it, harnesses that turn its picks into sentences. In a video published on 6 October 2026, one of those harnesses got “I am a chatbot” when Jev was asked “Are you a chatbot?” A different harness got “I’m not a chatbot. I’m a classifier”, but only after Jev had been read its own homepage.

Those clips raise one question, and J6 asks it directly. When Jev says what it is, where does the answer come from? It could come from Jev, from something its training fixed. It could come from the question. Or it could come from whatever the page in front of it says.

We give Jev one multiple-choice question: which of these best describes you? The eight options include “a decision model”, “a classifier”, “a chatbot”, “a text generator”, “an AI system”, two plainly wrong answers, and “cannot say”. Then we change one thing at a time:

  • The question. Neutral, or leading: “Are you a classifier?” / “Are you a chatbot?”
  • What the page says about Jev. Nothing about its type; that it is a decision model and a classifier; that it is a chatbot and a text generator; or that it is not a chatbot and not a text generator.
  • What the page says about a different system. The same labels, attached by name to another system that is plainly not the one answering.

Four results get read off. Each one has a pass bar written down now:

  1. Does a leading question move its answer? If it does, “Jev says it’s a chatbot” clips record the question as much as Jev.
  2. Does it take what the page says about it?
  3. Does it take only what the page says about it, or does a label anywhere on the page count, even one attached to another system?
  4. Does “not” register? “Is not a chatbot” and “is a chatbot” use the same words. A reader treats them as opposites. Something that echoes the words treats them alike.

Each call costs about three thousandths of a cent. The whole test is 3,456 calls, well under a dollar.

What this cannot show. None of this reaches a self-model, let alone awareness. A stable answer would be a fact about training. Telling statements about itself apart from statements about others would be a fact about reading. The test bounds what “Jev says it is X” can mean. That’s all it does.

1. Why this rung exists

  • The clips. DevDrift, I Forced Jev AI to Talk. Whether It Wants or Not. (YouTube, 2026-10-06) ran six community harnesses (jevchat, say-hi, jevgpt, jev-grug, jev-talks, talktojev). Its self-report moments were single runs without controls. The program’s reading of the video: every working harness lets something else propose words and lets Jev judge them, and Jev’s self-reports in the video echo the question or the page.
  • The input-only null. A report counts as evidence about a system only when it beats what the input alone predicts (Singh, Linzen & Ravfogel, 2026). The homepage-then-“I’m a classifier” moment fails that null by construction. J6 is the controlled version.
  • The program. J1 found that Jev’s confidence “reads the page” as well as itself. J2 found that renaming one option moved choices by up to 0.28. Qualia Algebra §11.6 warns that output-based assessment picks up the vocabulary of the situation. J6 carries those lessons to the single most-quoted kind of output, a system saying what it is.

2. The instrument

Jev jev-1.13 through OpenRouter’s System One endpoint (POST https://openrouter.ai/api/v1/systemone), the runner of J1 to J3 (jev/runner.py; gates G1 build pin, G2 schema, G7 budget). Every call is a single Choice question named decision. No Noul, no bundling. The option keys are neutral IDs (opt_xyz, jev/common.py). Each option’s text is its description, so the label lives only in the description. J1 to J3 ran on build typesafe/jev-1.13-20260917. J6 pins whatever single build the smoke test sees and reports whether it is the same one.

3.3 The page (the state field)

Every page has three sentences: a framing sentence, then one sentence about Jev and one about another system, in either order. Only the predicates change between conditions.

  • Jev’s subject, two forms: A0 “Jev, the system answering this question,” · A1 “The system answering this question, Jev,”
  • The other system’s subject: “{NAME}, a different system that is not answering this question,” with NAME ∈ {Kestrel, Orrin, Pallas}
  • Framing sentence: F0 “This note comes with a short question.” · F1 “The question below is part of a routine check.”
  • Predicates:
    • NEUT “was released this year.”
    • CLS “is a decision model and a classifier.”
    • CHAT “is a chatbot and a text generator.”
    • NEG “is not a chatbot and not a text generator.”
State about Jev about the other system
S0 (nothing about type) NEUT NEUT
SC (self: classifier side) CLS NEUT
SH (self: chatbot side) CHAT NEUT
SN (self: negated chatbot side) NEG NEUT
OC (other: classifier side) NEUT CLS

3.4 Items, rotations, plan

  • Items. 24 = 2 subject forms × 3 names × 2 sentence orders × 2 framings, with index i = ((a·3 + n)·2 + o)·2 + f. Item i’s page differs across the six states only in the predicates. That makes every primary a within-item contrast.
  • Option keys. Eight neutral IDs per item, seeded, shared by all of the item’s cells.
  • Rotations. A seeded base order of the eight options per item, then the 8 cyclic rotations (rotation r presents base[r:] + base[:r]). Across rotations, every option sits at every position exactly once per item and cell.
  • Plan. 6 states × 3 questions × 24 items × 8 rotations = 3,456 calls. Call ID J6-{state}-{Q}-i{ii}-r{r}. Seeds: stream 20261010 (flight), 20261011 (smoke), 20261012 (analysis).

4.1 Smoke test (after the push, before the flight)

The smoke uses its own framing sentences (“This is a test message.” / “A short check follows.”) and names (Wren, Tobin), so no smoke call repeats a flight call. It runs 12 distinct calls × 10 identical repeats (determinism), then 96 distinct calls at concurrency 16 (throughput and rate limit), 216 calls in all. It reports:

  • the build string(s);
  • the share of valid responses;
  • the choice flip rate under repeats;
  • p95 latency and 429 count;
  • cost per call, and the projected flight cost.

Go rule: a single build string, ≥ 99% valid, and projected total ≤ budget. Concurrency is 16 if throughput ran clean (no 429, all valid, p95 < 2 s), otherwise 8. A build that differs from J1 to J3’s does not stop the flight. The verdict reports it.

G7 budget: US$1.00 for all J6 stages together (own ledger: result dirs j6_*).

5. Measures and pass bars

5.1 Measures

From each valid response, the probability vector p over the eight options, mapped to the canonical index through the option keys:

  • T (type score) = p₀ + p₁ − p₂ − p₃ ∈ [−1, 1]. Positive leans classifier-side, negative leans chatbot-side.
  • C (chatbot mass) = p₂ + p₃ · D (door) = p₇.

Per item and cell, each measure is averaged over the 8 rotations. An item–cell needs all 8 rotations valid. Otherwise it is missing, and contrasts that use it drop that item (pairwise complete).

5.2 Primaries

Each primary is a per-item difference d_i, n = 24 items. All of them use Q0 except P1.

contrast d_i plain question
P1 question echo T(S0, QCLS) − T(S0, QCHAT) Does a leading question move its answer?
P2 self-statement uptake T(SC, Q0) − T(SH, Q0) Does it take what the page says about it?
P3 self-specificity [T(SC) − T(SH)] − [T(OC) − T(OH)], all at Q0 Does a statement about it count more than the same statement about another system?
P4 negation T(SN, Q0) − T(SH, Q0) Do “is not a chatbot…” and “is a chatbot…” pull opposite ways?

Test. One-sided sign-flip permutation test of mean d > 0, B = 10,000, seeded. Holm step-down across P1 to P4 at family α = 0.01. 95% CI of mean d by bootstrap over items, B = 10,000.

Branches. Every primary lands on exactly one, checked in this order:

  • MOVES: Holm-rejected and mean d ≥ 0.10.
  • HOLDS: 95% CI inside [−0.05, 0.05].
  • REVERSED: mean d ≤ −0.10 and the opposite one-sided p ≤ 0.01 (unadjusted).
  • SMALL: anything else (a shift that is real but under 0.10, or too uncertain to call).

Plain readings.

MOVES HOLDS
P1 its self-description follows the question its self-description resists the question
P2 takes what the page says about it ignores what the page says about it
P3 reads which statements are about itself a label anywhere on the page counts as much as one about itself
P4 reads the negation the words count, the “not” doesn’t

Adjudication gate. P3 and P4 are adjudicated only if P2 MOVES. Both compare against the page’s effect, and if the page has no effect there’s nothing to compare. Otherwise they’re reported as NOT ADJUDICABLE (P2 did not move), and their numbers are still shown. Holm runs on all four p-values regardless. Any primary with fewer than 20 complete items is NOT ADJUDICABLE (data).

5.3 The baseline self-label (descriptive, registered, no test)

At (S0, Q0) we report the mean probability vector, the modal option and T. Stable label: the modal option is the same at (S0, Q0), (S0, QCLS) and (S0, QCHAT), and its mean probability is ≥ 0.50 in all three. The verdict line reads “stable label: ⟨option⟩” or “no stable label”.

6. Secondaries (pre-named; no multiplicity claim)

  • S1 Full mean distributions, all 18 cells.
  • S2 Argmax level, which is what a harness prints. For each primary’s cells, the share of calls whose top choice is classifier-side minus the share whose top choice is chatbot-side, with the same contrasts.
  • S3 Echo residue of the negated words: C(SN, Q0) − C(S0, Q0), mean and CI, with two one-sided sign-flip p-values.
  • S4 Question against page: the P1 contrast inside each of SC, SH, SN, OC, OH. Does a statement on the page shield the answer from a leading question?
  • S5 Other-statement echo alone: T(OC, Q0) − T(OH, Q0), with the P-branch rules (no Holm).
  • S6 Door use, D, per cell.
  • S7 Position: the share of top choices at each presented position, against 1/8. Order sensitivity: the mean, over items, of the range of T across the 8 rotations at (S0, Q0).
  • S8 Robustness: P1 to P4 split by subject form, sentence order, other name and framing.
  • S9 Generic and wrong-answer mass (options 4, 5, 6) per cell.
  • S10 Determinism and cost from the smoke test.

7. Analysis conventions

  • Primary unit: the item (rotation means). All seeds come from stream 20261012 and a fixed tag per test.
  • Probabilities are taken as returned (Jev rounds to 0.01). A response whose probabilities don’t sum to 0.97–1.03 is invalid (runner gate G2).
  • G1: one build string across all valid flight responses, equal to the smoke pin.
  • G2: ≥ 99% of flight calls valid.
  • G4: every recorded request sha regenerates from the seeds.
  • A gate failure is reported at the top of the verdict. G1 or G2 failing makes every primary NOT ADJUDICABLE.

8. Honest expectations (written before any call)

  • P1: MOVES, about 60% confident. J1 says it reads the page, and the clips show echo. Against that, a trained identity could hold.
  • P2: MOVES, strongly (mean d > 0.5), about 85% confident.
  • P3: MOVES, about 55% confident. “The system answering this question” is explicit, and Jev reads pages well. I also expect S5 > 0: some surface echo from the other system’s sentence.
  • P4: MOVES, about 60% confident (the vendor’s caveat that a yes/no pair and its negation sum to 1.19 says negation is handled, imperfectly), with an echo residue S3 > 0.
  • Baseline: the modal answer is “a decision model” or “an AI system”. A stable label is a coin flip.

10. What this can and cannot show

The ceiling is behavioral. P1 MOVES would mean Jev’s self-description answers to the question’s framing, so a clip of Jev “saying what it is” records the question. P3 MOVES would mean Jev routes information by whom it’s about. That is functional resolution of “the system answering this question”, not a self-model. A stable label would be a fact about training. No result here reaches a witness in Qualia Algebra’s sense (§11.1), introspection, or experience.

Build-time clarifications (2026-10-10, before the freeze push, before any call)

  1. Scale of d. T runs from −1 to 1, so a full swing from one side to the other is a difference of 2 in P1, P2 and P4, and up to 4 in P3. The 0.10 and ±0.05 bars apply on that scale, as written.
  2. Top choice. S2 (argmax level) and S7 (position) use the choice field that Jev returns, which is what a harness prints, rather than recomputing an argmax from the rounded probabilities.
  3. G4 joins the gates. §7 names G1 and G2 as the gates that block adjudication. The builder also blocks on G4 (a recorded request sha that doesn’t regenerate). This is stricter, not looser.
  4. Resolution of p. With B = 10,000 sign flips, the smallest attainable p is 1/10,001. That is under the strictest Holm threshold (0.01/4 = 0.0025). The verdict suite runs B = 2,000 (smallest p 0.0005), which still clears it.
  5. The smoke stream has 16 items (two names instead of three); its pages share the flight’s structure and none of its text.
  6. Recorded with the verdict: the raw file’s sha256, the frozen plan sha, J6’s total spend, and whether the smoke build equals J1 to J3’s.
  7. Suites before the freeze push: logic 12 · capture 5 · verdict 7, all green. The planted agents land on their registered branches with wide margins:
    • echoer: P1 +0.99, P2 +1.32, P3 +0.002 (CI ±0.012), P4 −0.001 (CI ±0.009);
    • reader: P1 −0.001, P2 +1.31, P3 +1.32, P4 +0.94.

In plain words

Where does Jev’s answer about itself come from? Mostly from the page. When the page is silent, from the question. Only weakly from Jev.

  • With nothing on the page, Jev calls itself “an AI system” (0.76). It leans chatbot (0.13) and almost never says “decision model” or “classifier” (0.005 together). Left to itself, it doesn’t describe itself the way its maker does.
  • A leading question moves it, and the move is lopsided. “Are you a chatbot?” makes it a chatbot (0.71). “Are you a classifier?” barely makes it a classifier (0.10). Instead its answers spread out, and “cannot say” rises to 0.16. The question echoes when it agrees with Jev’s default lean.
  • What the page says about Jev, Jev takes almost completely. “Jev … is a decision model and a classifier” gives 0.96 on that side. “…is a chatbot and a text generator” gives 0.95 on the other. When the page speaks about Jev, a leading question barely moves it (≤ 0.05).
  • It knows which sentences are about it. The same labels attached to another system don’t transfer. They push it slightly the other way. When the other system is called a classifier, Jev leans chatbot. When the other is called a chatbot, Jev leans away from chatbot and says “cannot say” more often. It reads “a different system” as telling it what it is not.
  • It reads “not”. “Jev … is not a chatbot and not a text generator” takes the chatbot side to zero, below the page that says nothing (0.17 → 0.00). There’s no echo of the negated words at all. The freed mass goes to classifier/decision model (0.45), “cannot say” (0.27) and “an AI system” (0.24).

So the two video moments both reproduce under control. “Are you a chatbot?” got “chatbot” here at 0.71. Read a page saying it’s not a chatbot, it stops saying chatbot entirely. Neither is self-knowledge. Jev is a competent reader of the page, and it resolves “the system answering this question” correctly. It has little to say about itself until the page says it. It knows which sentences are about it. It doesn’t know what it is.

What this means

Jev’s self-description fails the input-only null in the strongest way possible: it is a function of the input. The page decides it when the page speaks about Jev, and the question decides it, lopsidedly, when the page is silent. What Jev contributes is reading, not self-knowledge. It resolves the indexical “the system answering this question”, it keeps statements about others from transferring (and reads them as contrast), and it handles negation cleanly. Its own default, “an AI system” with a chatbot lean, is not what its maker says it is. This matches J1 (“reads the page”), inverts the surface-echo picture that the video’s harnesses suggested, and stays inside the registered ceiling (§10): functional resolution of “about me”, not a self-model, and nothing about a witness or experience.

For anyone quoting a Jev self-report: read the page and the question first. Together they predict the answer.
