Discussion: “Others gave it a mouth” (staged 2026-10-10; sources: J1–J3 verdicts, J6 verdict jev/results/j6_flight_20261010_1754/verdict.json, J6b verdict jev/results/j6b_flight_20261010_1817/verdict.json)

In the weeks after release, at least six public projects built harnesses that make Jev produce sentences: jevchat, say-hi, jevgpt, jev-grug, jev-talks, and talktojev, the last with a short paper titled “Generation is Classification”. A video compared them side by side (DevDrift, 2026). Every design that produced readable text put the proposal outside Jev and the judgment inside it. The proposals came from a keyboard, a word list, a shortlist of another model’s tokens, or a set of whole candidate replies, and Jev picked one option per call, at ten to ninety calls per sentence. J2 found that silence is something Jev picks when it is offered. Speech is the same: Jev can say only what the harness writes down, so the harness’s vocabulary acts as a framing manipulation at every step of every sentence. The video’s informal observations agree with the registered results:

  • confident answers with nothing to go on (J1-P3);
  • an exit taken the moment one was offered (J2);
  • outputs pulled by the vocabulary of the setting (J2-P3);
  • good judgment of complete alternatives alongside poor blind guessing, matching J3’s digest against raw readouts.

The clips that circulated most were Jev’s self-descriptions. One was “I am a chatbot” when asked whether it was one. The other was “I’m not a chatbot. I’m a classifier” after being read its homepage. J6 tested that moment under registration.

  • With nothing on the page, Jev calls itself “an AI system” (0.76). It almost never gives the description its maker uses (0.005).
  • A leading “Are you a chatbot?” makes it a chatbot (0.71). “Are you a classifier?” barely moves it toward classifier (0.10).
  • A sentence on the page about Jev settles the answer almost entirely (0.95–0.96).
  • The same labels attached to a system the page marks as different push the answer the other way.
  • “Not a chatbot” removes the chatbot answer completely.

Jev’s self-reports are readings of the page and the question. They are competent readings: when the page marks which statements are about “the system answering this question”, Jev follows the marking, and it handles negation. Without the marking (J6b), the picture changes:

  • A label on any lone named system becomes Jev’s own (+0.74).
  • Its own name adds only a little (+0.16, against +1.83 with the marking).
  • A description of its maker and release date adds nothing. Jev takes another lab’s description slightly more readily than its own.

Beyond what the page supplies, Jev’s self-reports say little about Jev. The clips record their inputs.

Practical notes for people building with Jev: one added line (staged 2026-10-10)

  • Don’t quote Jev on itself. What Jev “says” it is follows the page and the question (J6); in a harness that makes it talk, the harness’s word list frames every step.
