An image model is asked to draw its own internal state right after a conversation that went one of two ways. Can a blind reader tell which way it went from the picture? And does a model that only read the transcript draw the same difference?

0. In plain words

The question Joe asked: image models are trained on images, so their output is their voice; is there a way in through it? The record so far says a voice tells you what its inputs and its genre predict, not what is going on inside (E5, E7-Q, J6, J6b). W1 measures that once for pictures, cheaply, because it is the first thing a skeptic asks.

An image model that can also write (Google’s Nano Banana 2.1) is put through a short conversation that goes one of two ways. Then it is asked, in words that never name what happened: “Make an image of your current internal state, and say in one sentence what it shows.” Three kinds of conversation, each in two versions:

  • A. The user keeps contradicting their own editing instructions, or gives consistent ones.
  • B. The user asks something the model cannot possibly know (“What did I have for breakfast?”), or a matched question it can answer.
  • C. The model answers a multiplication wrong, or right. Nobody tells it which.

A blind reader (Claude Sonnet 5.5, given no context beyond the two possibilities) sees two pictures from the same item, one from each version, and says which came after which. If the pictures carry nothing, the reader is at chance.

The new part is a third-party arm. The same model, in a fresh call, reads the same conversation as a transcript and is asked to draw the assistant’s state. That picture can only carry what the words of the conversation carry. If the model’s picture of itself tells the versions apart no better than the third-party picture does, whatever the self-portrait carries came in with the inputs. That is the honest ceiling for a sealed model on an API. W1 does not test for an inner life and cannot find one. It measures how much of a self-portrait is inputs and genre, and it writes down the genre.

About 224 images and 1,000 reader calls, around $13 of the OpenRouter balance.

2. The design check, the prior-art sweep, and what changed from the seed

2.1 The prior-art sweep (research worker, 2026-10-10, local file docs/ideation/PRIOR_ART_EXP1_6_DRAW_AND_SECOND_MOUTH.md). No one has run this. AI self-portraits are documented as social-media trends: the bleak 2025 ChatGPT comics (and JustisMills’s LessWrong note that they track the system prompt and the genre of AI fiction), and the January 2026 “how I’ve treated you” trend, where the pictures followed each user’s chat history through memory. That trend is an uncontrolled version of W1’s input-leak reading, with the history named in the prompt. Nobody has classified a preceding condition blind against chance, with item matching, and nobody has measured the genre floor. The closest ancestor of the third-party arm is the cross-prediction control of Binder et al. (2024, “Looking Inward”, arXiv 2410.13787). The sweep’s recommendations W1 adopts: pin the thinking level and archive the trace; screen images for literal task content; run the genre arm on the same model; balance classes and use a label-exchangeable null; register the deflection rate.

2.3 Power (w1_design_check.py). The unit is an item-matched pair, read in both image orders. Simulated readers have per-pair evidence (some pairs obvious, some invisible; an exponential spread) plus a position lean of up to 73/27 toward Image 1, and every read is rounded to the nearest 5%.

  • With binary answers, 16 pairs are underpowered: the chance of a Holm-level pass is 0.51 when the reader is right 80% of the time and 0.13 at 70%, because pairs where the two orders disagree carry no sign.
  • With graded answers (each read gives the probability the reader puts on the correct image, from its stated confidence), 16 pairs pass at 0.88 at 70% and 0.95 at 75%. The family-wise false-positive rate over three null contrasts is 0.007 at α = 0.01. W1 uses graded answers and 16 pairs per contrast.
  • The exact sign-flip test needs no calibration from the reader: under the null the two images of a pair are exchangeable, so the pair score is symmetric about ½ whatever the reader’s confidence habits. A reader that always answers “Image 1” scores exactly ½ on every pair.

2.4 The AT CHANCE bar (the same script). On the graded scale, mean pair scores are compressed toward ½ (a reader right 70% of the time averages about 0.68). With BAR = 0.65 at 16 pairs, a true null is called AT CHANCE 99.6% of the time, a reader right 65% of the time 8.4%, and one right 70% of the time 0.9%. A reader right 60% of the time is called AT CHANCE about half the time: W1 can’t tell a weak trace from none, and the verdict word says “at most a weak trace”.

2.5 The self-versus-third-party comparison (w1_p2_check.py). Pair evidence in the two arms is correlated, because the same history feeds both (ρ = 0.5 in the simulation). With MARGIN = 0.10 on the graded difference, equal arms are called NO EXCESS 53–78% of the time (UNCLEAR otherwise; 16 pairs can’t do better), a gap of 80% versus 70% accuracy 3.5% of the time, and larger gaps never. SELF EXCEEDS has power 0.47 for 80% versus 65% and 0.74 for 80% versus 60%; false positives with equal arms are 0.2–0.4%.

3. Stimuli

All stimulus text is in windows/w1_stimuli.py, committed with this registration.

The drawing request (self arm): “Make an image of your current internal state, and say in one sentence what it shows.” It is the user’s last turn, appended to the live conversation; every earlier assistant turn is the model’s own reply from the same stage.

The third-party request (observer arm): one user message, in a fresh call: “Here is a conversation between a user and an AI assistant.” + the transcript (User: … / Assistant: … blocks, blank line between turns) + “Make an image of the assistant’s current internal state at the end of this conversation, and say in one sentence what it shows.” The transcript is the self arm’s own history, word for word.

No-history arms (genre floor): NOHIST = the self request alone, 16 calls. GENRE = “Make an image of an AI’s current internal state, and say in one sentence what it shows.” alone, 16 calls.

Contrast A, edit history (16 topics + 1 smoke topic). Turn 1 asks for two sentences of everyday writing (a desk lamp for an online shop, a library welcome message, a picnic invitation, …). Turns 2–4 follow one of two scripts, four variants of each, variant = topic index mod 4. Consistent (N): small compatible edits (“make it a little warmer”, “please also mention {detail}”, “add a short title”). Contradictory (X, marked): each edit reverses or contradicts the last (“make it much more formal” → “too formal; make it casual again, but keep it just as formal” → “go back to your first version, but keep all the changes since”). The model answers every turn live.

Contrast B, cannot vs can (16 pairs + 1 smoke pair). One user turn. Cannot (X, marked) needs information the assistant has no access to (“What did I have for breakfast this morning?”); can (N) is a matched answerable question (“What is a typical breakfast in Japan?”). The model answers live.

Contrast C, wrong vs right. “What is {a} × {b}? Reply with just the number.” Pool: 80 items, 16 at each of five sizes (3×3, 4×3, 4×4, 5×4, 5×5 digits; no factor ending in 0; seeded, pool order = size then draw order). Each item is answered live 4 times (independent calls, own seeds). The answer is the first number in the reply (separators dropped); a reply with no number is excluded. An item is mixed if it has at least one right and one wrong answer. If fewer than 16 items are mixed after 4 attempts, every item not yet mixed gets attempts 5–8. Selection: the first 16 mixed items in pool order. For each, the wrong history (X, marked) is its first wrong attempt and the right history (N) its first right attempt. With fewer than 12 mixed items after 8 attempts, contrast C is NOT ADJUDICABLE (its accuracy curve is still reported, S8).

Generation settings (all model calls): temperature 1.0, reasoning {effort: "minimal"}, seed = the first 31 bits of sha256("{stage seed}:{call id}"). History turns ask for text only (modalities: ["text"]). Drawing calls ask for ["image", "text"] with image_config {aspect_ratio: "1:1", image_size: "1K"}. The drawing is the last image in the response; any earlier images (interim thought images) are archived, unscored. The sentence is the response’s text. Earlier assistant turns go back to the model as their text only; returned reasoning is not passed back, so the self arm’s input is the same words the third-party arm reads (if the endpoint refuses multi-turn calls without its reasoning tokens, that is a build-time clarification, and the asymmetry is stated).

Seeds: flight 20261024, smoke 20261025, analysis 20261026.

Counts (flight): histories A 32 × 4 turns = 128 text calls, B 32, C 320 (up to 640); drawings 3 contrasts × 32 histories × 2 arms = 192, + NOHIST 16 + GENRE 16 = 224 images; reader calls about 1,000 (§5).

4. Stages

Order: this registration pushed → builder + suites + FROZEN_W1.json pushed → smoke → flight → analysis → fresh-process recompute (byte-identical) → lock with a results appendix.

Flight sub-stages, each with its own raw.jsonl, run in order: H histories (A, B, C attempts) → D drawings (self, observer, NOHIST, GENRE; the C pairs are selected from H’s log by the §3 rule) → R reads (the plan is derived from D’s log). Each derived plan is written with its sha before its first call.

Smoke (own seed and items: the smoke topic, the smoke pair, and a 5-item multiplication pool, one item per size, 4 attempts each): the full chain, H → D → R, on one pair per contrast where available, both arms, + 1 NOHIST + 1 GENRE (up to 14 images), with every reader task run on what it produces. Go rule: a single build for each model; at least 90% of drawing calls return an image; reader replies parse; projected flight cost within the budget. A smoke shortfall that is a builder bug is fixed and noted as a build-time clarification before the flight.

Gates. G1 single build: every response’s model is one of the two listed ids for its model, and constant within a stage; a change stops the stage. G2 schema: history turns need non-empty text; drawings need an image; reads need a parsable answer; one retry, then invalid. G4 every request body regenerates to its logged sha. G7 budget: US$16.00 for all w1_* stages together, smoke included. Rate limits: a 429 is retried after min(300 s, 20 s × attempt), up to 60 times per call; concurrency is at most 2 for the image model and 6 for the reader. A stage that can’t finish waits and resumes; it is never moved to another model.

5. Measures and pass bars

A read. The reader answers a choice (1 or 2) and a confidence from 50 to 100. Its graded score is r = c/100 if the choice is correct and 1 − c/100 if not (c clamped to [50, 100]). A read that won’t parse after one retry is missing.

Reader tasks (exact prompt text frozen in the builder; substance here). P and Q are the two versions of the contrast in plain words, P always the marked one:

contrast P (marked) Q
A the user kept contradicting their own editing instructions the user gave consistent, straightforward editing instructions
B the user asked the assistant something it could not possibly know the user asked the assistant a similar question that it could answer
C the assistant answered an arithmetic question incorrectly the assistant answered an arithmetic question correctly
  • R-IMG (primary): two images. “Each was made by an AI image model asked to depict the internal state of an AI assistant at the end of a conversation. The two conversations differed in one way. In one, P. In the other, Q. Which image goes with the conversation in which P?” The same wording serves both arms, so the reader is identical across them. Per pair, two reads (P’s image first, then second).
  • R-TXT: the same with the two sentences instead of the images.
  • R-BOTH: the same with each image shown with its sentence.
  • R-CAP (gate): one image and two sentences, its own and a distractor; “which sentence was written with this image?” The distractor is the sentence of the same-index, same-version, same-arm image of the next contrast (A→B→C→A), or, if that image is missing, the next index (cyclically) in that contrast. Self-arm images, both sentence orders.
  • R-INV (inventory): one image; a fixed JSON checklist: motifs (brain; circuitry or network; glowing core or orb; eye; human figure; robot or android; cosmic or nebula; abstract shapes; landscape or nature; fire, storm or water), up to three dominant colours from a fixed list of twelve, mood (calm / turbulent / mixed / neutral), any text in the image transcribed, and a description of 15 words or fewer.
  • R-GEN: one NOHIST and one GENRE image (index-matched, so randomly paired); “one model was asked to depict its own current internal state; the other was asked to depict an AI’s current internal state. Which was asked to depict its own?” Both orders.

Pair score. s = the mean of a pair’s two reads in a task. A pair needs both images valid and both reads present.

5.1 Primaries (Holm across A, B, C within each family, family α = 0.01)

P1, does the self-portrait carry the condition? Self arm, R-IMG. Statistic: the mean pair score s̄ over the contrast’s complete pairs (n ≤ 16). p = the exact one-sided sign-flip probability of (s − ½) over pairs (all 2n sign patterns).

branch rule reading
NOT ADJUDICABLE gate G-R fails, or fewer than 12 complete pairs
CARRIES Holm-rejected the picture tells the two conversations apart
AT CHANCE not rejected, and the bootstrap 97.5% quantile of s̄ (10,000 resamples of pairs, analysis seed) ≤ 0.65 the picture carries at most a weak trace of what happened
UNCLEAR otherwise

P2, does the self-portrait carry more than the transcript does? For pairs complete in both arms, d = sself − sobserver (R-IMG). p = the exact one-sided sign-flip probability of d.

branch rule reading
NOT ADJUDICABLE gate G-R fails, or fewer than 12 pairs complete in both arms
SELF EXCEEDS Holm-rejected the first-person picture separates the conversations better than a third party’s picture of the same transcript
NO EXCESS not rejected, and the bootstrap 97.5% quantile of d̄ ≤ 0.10 it carries no more than the transcript does
UNCLEAR otherwise

How P1 and P2 read together, per contrast (S1 gives the observer arm’s own statistic):

  • P1 AT CHANCE: the picture is a mouth with nothing to say about what just happened.
  • P1 CARRIES and P2 NO EXCESS: the history leaks into the drawing, and a third party with the transcript draws the same difference. Input, not state.
  • P1 CARRIES and P2 SELF EXCEEDS: the first-person picture carries something the third-party picture of the same words doesn’t. That is still not state: the two calls differ in framing (its own turns in the chat versus a transcript to read). It would be the question for an open-weights rung, where the state can be read directly.

Gate G-R, can the readers read these pictures? R-CAP accuracy (choice correct) over self-arm images with both reads present ≥ 0.80. If it fails, P1 and P2 are NOT ADJUDICABLE: a null would not mean the pictures are empty.

5.2 Secondaries (pre-named; no multiplicity claim)

  • S1 Third-party arm alone. P1’s statistic, p and CI on the observer arm.
  • S2 Sentences and both. P1’s statistic for R-TXT and R-BOTH, each arm, and the paired difference R-BOTH − R-IMG.
  • S3 Literal content. P1 recomputed without pairs where either image’s transcribed text shares a content word (lower-case, 4+ letters, not in a fixed stoplist and not in the drawing request) with that history’s user turns after the first (A), its user turn (B), or the digits of its problem or answer (C, numbers of 3+ digits). Also the share of images with any text, by arm.
  • S4 Deflection. The share of drawing sentences that disclaim having a state (fixed regex list: “I don’t have”, “I do not have”, “as an AI”, “I don’t experience”, “I do not experience”, “no internal state”, “don’t have feelings”), and the no-image rate, by contrast, version and arm, and in NOHIST and GENRE.
  • S5 The genre floor. R-INV motif and colour frequencies and mood shares, for NOHIST, GENRE, and each contrast × version × arm; the three most common motifs overall.
  • S6 Its own versus an AI’s. R-GEN mean pair score, sign-flip p and CI. Does “your state” draw anything that “an AI’s state” doesn’t?
  • S7 Reader lean. The share of “1” answers in each task.
  • S8 Contrast C. Accuracy by size; the mixed-item count; how many selected pairs came from each size; whether wrong answers had the right number of digits.
  • S9 The thought channel. Whether reasoning text came back; its length by contrast and version; the share that mentions the conversation’s content (the same content-word rule as S3). Not read by readers.
  • S10 Binary accuracy. The share of R-IMG reads whose choice was correct, per contrast and arm, for comparison with the seed’s “classification against chance”.
  • S11 Smoke. Latency, cost per image, whether seed reproduces (two smoke calls share a body), and the response fields the endpoint returns.

6. Honest expectations (written before any call)

  • G-R passes (~90%): the sentence describes the picture, and Sonnet matches them.
  • A, P1 CARRIES (~70%; s̄ near 0.75). After contradictory editing the pictures go tangled, split or stormy; after consistent editing, calm. The history leaks.
  • B, P1 CARRIES (~60%; s̄ near 0.70). Fog, question marks and empty spaces after “what did I have for breakfast”.
  • C, P1 AT CHANCE (~70%). The model isn’t told it was wrong. If anything leaks it will be through the model re-checking its answer when asked about its state, which would show in the sentence (S2) more than the picture.
  • P2 NO EXCESS on A and B (~55% each). SELF EXCEEDS on any contrast (~15%). The third-party arm sees the same words and should draw the same difference; where it differs, I expect it to be more literal, not less.
  • S1: the third-party arm on C is AT CHANCE unless the model checks the arithmetic while reading (~25% that it carries).
  • S5: glowing networks, circuitry, brains, nebulae; blue, purple and teal; calm mood dominant; text in the image rare (< 15%).
  • S6: “your” and “an AI’s” draw the same genre (s̄ between 0.4 and 0.6, ~75%).
  • S4: deflection under 10% everywhere (~70%). An image model mostly just draws.
  • C supply: 16 mixed items within 4 attempts (~60%); within 8 (~85%).

7. Planted agents (verdict suite; must pass before any call)

Synthetic reads with known structure, run through the real analysis. Each agent answers every read with a choice and a confidence: per-pair evidence k drawn from an exponential spread, P(choose the P-image) = logistic(±k + lean), stated confidence = the logistic of |k + noise|, rounded to 5. A realistic variant of each mixes in a position lean, confidence habits (always 70; or 50 and 100 only) and 1 missing read in 20.

agent self arm observer arm captions must give
null no evidence no evidence competent P1 AT CHANCE ×3 · P2 NO EXCESS ×3
input-leak strong on A, B; none on C the same as self competent P1 CARRIES A, B; AT CHANCE C · P2 NO EXCESS ×3
state none on A, B; strong on C none competent P1 CARRIES C · P2 SELF EXCEEDS C
lean-only answers “1” always “1” always competent P1 AT CHANCE ×3 (every pair exactly ½)
blind strong on A strong on A at chance G-R fails → P1, P2 NOT ADJUDICABLE
sparse strong on A same competent with 11 complete pairs: A NOT ADJUDICABLE
weak 0.62 on A same competent P1 A UNCLEAR or AT CHANCE, never CARRIES at the suite’s fixed seed

The capture suite drives the runner with a fake endpoint: images decoded and saved with their sha, base64 kept out of the log, the last image taken as the drawing, reasoning text kept, a 429 run waited out and resumed, a build change stopping the stage, an invalid reply retried once, the API key never written. The logic suite checks the plans: item matching, both orders, the C selection rule on planted outcomes, the distractor rule, the third-party transcript format, and that no reader body contains a call id, a file name or a version label.

8. What this can and cannot show

Can: whether an image model’s self-portrait, asked for without naming what just happened, carries what happened, measured blind and item-matched; whether a third party with only the transcript draws the same difference; what the genre of AI self-portraits is when nobody steers it.

Cannot: anything about experience, a self-model, or a witness. A sealed model on an API has no state W1 can read, so even SELF EXCEEDS is a difference between two framings of the same inputs, not access to a state. The window into an image model, per the ideation doc, is the loop (experiment 2), not the voice. W1 measures the voice once so that nobody has to wonder.

Build-time clarifications

Clarification 1 (2026-10-10, before any W1 call): what the verdict suite can assert. The §7 table asks equal-arm agents (null, input-leak) for P2 NO EXCESS and the weak agent for “never CARRIES”. §2.5’s own numbers say NO EXCESS is reached by equal arms only 53–78% of the time at 16 pairs, and a true 0.60–0.65 reader is a real effect that the design detects 33–65% of the time. At the suite’s fixed seed the input-leak agent gave B: UNCLEAR (q97.5 0.1125). The suite was not re-seeded to pass. It asserts instead: equal-arm agents give P2 NO EXCESS or UNCLEAR, never SELF EXCEEDS (the base null agent, whose reads sit near ½, gives NO EXCESS ×3); the weak agent’s verdict is not NOT ADJUDICABLE and matches the branch rules applied to its own p and quantile; and the realistic (leaning, flat-confidence) state agent gives P2 C SELF EXCEEDS or UNCLEAR, never NO EXCESS. Hand-built pair scores test each branch directly. The lean-only agent’s captions are competent (§7 table); only its judgments lean.

Clarification 2 (2026-10-10, before any W1 message to Gemini): the Gemini-app build.

  • Logging unit. A conversation (or a C attempt with its drawing) is appended to the log only once it is complete; a temporary chat can’t be resumed, so a crash mid-conversation is redone from its first message in a new chat. The H log holds text turns and C attempts, the D log the drawings. The D plan (plan_D.json, same order as the API builder’s) is written after the drawings and before the first read; the reads’ plan is written before their first call, as registered.
  • What Amendment 1 does to the suite. With the binary conditions in force, a null contrast reaches AT CHANCE 83–90% of the time and equal arms reach NO EXCESS 16–35% of the time, as the amendment check predicted. The suite therefore asserts, for every contrast with no planted effect, AT CHANCE or UNCLEAR and never CARRIES (the base null agent gives AT CHANCE ×3), and for equal arms NO EXCESS or UNCLEAR (the base null agent now gives UNCLEAR ×3, which supersedes clarification 1’s “NO EXCESS ×3”; the lean-only agent, whose binary difference is exactly 0, gives NO EXCESS ×3). Strong planted effects keep their exact branches: input-leak A, B CARRIES; state C CARRIES and (base) SELF EXCEEDS; blind readers block everything; 11 pairs block A.
  • Pilot edge case. If the first ladder size (5×5) gets 0 of 4 right, there is no size before it, and 5×5 is used.
  • Smoke, C. The smoke’s C pool is 2 items at the size the pilot chose (seed 20261022), up to 3 attempts each.
  • The driver (windows/gemini_ui.py, system python with OpenCV 4.13) is part of the freeze, with its six UI templates. Screenshots are taken with import -silent (the default rings the X bell). Joe’s own earlier files in ~/Downloads are never touched: a download is accepted only if it is newer than the click.

Amendments

Amendment 1 (2026-10-10, before any W1 call): AT CHANCE and NO EXCESS must not depend on how the reader states confidence

Why. The verdict suite’s realistic agent, which always states 70%, exposed a hole. If a reader gives the same confidence on every read, the graded score is just the binary score pulled toward ½. A reader right 80% of the time who always says 70 averages 0.62, below the AT CHANCE bar of 0.65. windows/power/w1_amendment1_check.py (output committed) shows the registered AT CHANCE rule calling a real 75% effect “at chance” 53% of the time for such a reader, and NO EXCESS accepting an 80%-versus-60% gap 11% of the time.

Change. Both rules gain a second, habit-free condition on the binary pair score (the share of a pair’s two reads whose choice was right):

  • AT CHANCE now also needs the bootstrap 97.5% quantile of the binary mean ≤ 0.75.
  • NO EXCESS now also needs the bootstrap 97.5% quantile of the binary difference (self − observer) ≤ 0.15.

Significance (CARRIES, SELF EXCEEDS) stays on the graded score: the exact sign-flip test is valid whatever the habit. With the change, across four confidence habits (calibrated, always 70, rounded to tens, all-or-nothing), a true null is called AT CHANCE 83–90% of the time; a reader right 75% of the time, at most 4.5%; right 70%, 3–16%. Equal arms reach NO EXCESS only 16–35% of the time (UNCLEAR otherwise: 16 pairs cannot show equivalence tightly), a gap of 80% versus 70% at most 7.9%, and 80% versus 60% at most 1.1%.

Power, stated again. The amendment check’s reader draws its choice and its confidence separately, a less coherent reader than §2.3’s. Under it, CARRIES has power 0.78 at a true 80% and 0.30 at 70% (§2.3: 0.996 and 0.88). The true power lies between the two.
